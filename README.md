Extending an X-ray &amp; Mid-Infrared diagnostic for absorption in Active Galactic Nuclei to higher redshifts using NuSTAR and XMM-Newton  
Ryan J. Turney, Claire L. Greenwell, David M. Alexander  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18286v1  
Published: 2026-11-13T15:14:03Z  
Determining the Compton thick fraction ($f_{CT}$) within the population of Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) provides insight into the evolution and growth of Supermassive Black Holes. Measuring $f_{CT}$ requires methods of modelling the AGN column density ($N_{\mathrm{H}}$), typically through spectral fitting which requires high source counts, or estimation methods such as the band ratio. We extend an $N_{\mathrm{H}}$ estimation method using the ratio of observed X-ray and MIR luminosity ($\log\left({L}_{2-10\mathrm{keV}} / {L}_{12\mathrm{μm}}\right)$), previously applied in the local universe ($z&lt;0.1$), to a higher redshift. We used a sample of 47 bright AGN from the NuSTAR 80-month catalogue (NSS80) with a median redshift of $\langle z \rangle = 0.35$ as the high redshift comparison, and we applied X-ray spectral fitting to the NuSTAR sources with physically motivated AGN models to provide $N_\mathrm{H}$ constraints. The high-$z$ relationship was found to be consistent with the same model as the low-$z$ comparison, but with modestly different parameters which we determined to be due to a higher X-ray luminosity within the NSS80 sample. We applied the model to the full NSS80 catalogue to determine a statistical measure of the observed Compton thick fraction at $f_{CT}=4.9^{+2.0}_{-2.3}\%$ to $1σ$ uncertainty, consistent with other recent low-$z$ Swift/BAT measurements.  

Magnetic kink instability of jet-wind outflows. I. Static column configuration  
Krishna Kumar, Serguei S. Komissarov  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18268v1  
Published: 2026-11-13T15:01:23Z  
We describe the results of computer simulations investigating the stability of an axisymmetric static equilibrium combining a highly magnetised ($σ=10^3$) central column and a mildly ($σ=0.1$) to weakly ($σ=0.001$) magnetised outer shell with a helical and mostly force-free magnetic field of constant pitch. The measured linear growth rates of the instability are in agreement with the maximum growth rate for a uniform density column with a force-free magnetic field of constant pitch obtained by Appl et al. (2000), being only a little bit higher. At the non-linear phase, the interface separating the central column and the outer shell develops the characteristic corkscrew shape, with the space inside and between the coils of the corkscrew filled by the shell plasma. In high-resolution runs, a secondary instability is seen as well. It has the form of helical protuberances of low-$σ$ plasma intruding inside the corkscrew coils. These appear to be aligned with the local magnetic field lines. We tentatively identify the secondary instability as the magnetic Rayleigh-Taylor instability. Overall, the results indicate that the current-driven kink instability may provide the baryon-loading of AGN jets suggested by the observations.  

Constraining Accretion Regimes of Little Red Dots from Their Variability  
Indu K. Dihingia  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18153v1  
Published: 2026-11-13T13:28:24Z  
The ``little red dots'' (LRDs) are photometrically and spectroscopically non-variable on rest-frame baselines of months, with a $5.9σ$ deficit relative to ordinary active galactic nuclei (AGNs). If we consider LRDs to be accreting black holes, this non-varying could be a clue about the nature of the accretion. Here, we present a general semi-analytic model of variability from super-Eddington (slim) accretion disks. We consider three mechanisms that play crucial roles in the observed variability, viz., advective attenuation, photospheric averaging, and photon diffusion filter. With this, above $\mdot \sim$ a few, the fractional rms collapses steeply with $\mdot$. Comparing with JWST monitoring constraints, we find that the non-detection of LRD variability requires $\mdot \gtrsim 30$ for $M_{\rm BH}\sim10^{7}\,M_\odot$ on a 0.5\,yr rest-frame baseline; a Monte Carlo forward model of the TWINKLE non-detections of 18 LRDs sharpens this to $\mdot\gtrsim60$ at 95\% confidence. On current baselines, these non-detections remain formally degenerate with a frozen sub-Eddington disk ($\mdot\lesssim0.1$). Longer monitoring will separate the two regimes as the frozen-disk variability grows with baseline, while the super-Eddington collapse does not. The same framework predicts strongly suppressed X-ray variability, testable with future X-ray observatories.  

WISE-VAULT: Decade-long Mid-Infrared Variability of WISE AGN  
Suvendu Rakshit  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17996v1  
Published: 2026-11-13T10:23:54Z  
Mid-infrared (MIR) variability provides a powerful probe of accretion processes and dust reprocessing in active galactic nuclei (AGNs), as it is less affected by extinction than optical flux. Using multi-epoch photometry from \textit{WISE} and NEOWISE spanning over a decade, we investigate the MIR variability of $\sim$4.5 million color-selected AGNs. Variability is quantified using maximum--minimum amplitudes ($ΔW1$), excess variance, reduced $χ^2$, and structure function (SF) analysis over timescales of 0.5--10 years. We identify 632,939 variable AGNs, including 54,897 sources that exhibit large-amplitude variability ($ΔW1 &gt; 0.5$ mag) and 624 with extreme intrinsic variability ($A_{\rm var} &gt; 0.5$ mag). A subset of these sources shows flare-like behavior, identifying them as candidate changing-look AGNs or tidal disruption events that warrant further spectroscopic and multi-wavelength follow-up. The SF analysis shows radio-detected AGNs exhibit the highest variability amplitudes, followed by X-ray detected and the overall AGN sample. The variability amplitude is strongly anti-correlated with the Eddington ratio, indicating enhanced variability in lower accretion rate systems. A weak positive dependence on radio-loudness further suggests a contribution from non-thermal emission in radio sources. These results demonstrate that MIR variability provides a robust diagnostic of accretion physics and an efficient means of identifying extreme and rapidly evolving AGN phenomena. The full catalog of variable AGNs is made publicly available.  

Soft Excess Variability and a Test of X-ray Reprocessing in AGN Mrk 359 and NGC 4051 using NICER and TESS  
Tejas Sewak, Michael M. Fausnaugh, Edward M. Cackett, Javier A. García, Keith Horne, Rahul Jayaraman, Viraja C. Khatu, Ethan Partington, Raj Prince  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17439v1  
Published: 2026-11-12T19:11:08Z  
Continuum reverberation mapping of AGN accretion disks assumes that X-ray reprocessing drives the UV/optical variations on short timescales. This reprocessing should also produce an X-ray reflection spectrum, which could be the soft excess observed in many Type 1 AGN. Consequently, if the soft excess traces the reflected emission, the soft excess and optical light curves should be correlated because their variability is driven by the same illuminating X-ray continuum. We present results from a cross-correlation study using simultaneous NICER and TESS observations of $\sim$$30\mathrm{~days}$ for two AGN: Mrk 359 and NGC 4051. We performed time-resolved spectroscopic fits to the X-ray data using a model with power-law and soft-excess components. We find no strong correlation between the optical and soft excess light curves, with correlation coefficients $R(τ)$ $\sim$0.5 for both sources. The total X-ray light curve also shows a similarly poor correlation with the optical variations. However, we find a stronger correlation between the primary X-ray flux and the soft excess flux for both sources. The lack of correlation between the primary X-rays and soft-excess with the optical variations implies that simple linear X-ray reprocessing is not the dominant process driving the optical variability. Either an intervening reprocessor between the inner X-ray region and outer disc is present that dilutes the X-ray-optical correlation, or the optical variability is driven by other mechanisms such as extreme-UV reprocessing or intrinsic disk variability.
