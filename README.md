A TESS Search for Optical Variability in Intermediate-mass Black Hole Candidates  
Jinghuai Yao, Wenwen Zuo, Hengxiao Guo, Ashutosh Tripathi  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27890v1  
Published: 2026-11-20T17:29:39Z  
Optical variability of active galactic nuclei (AGNs) probes accretion onto black holes. We study the optical variability of AGNs in the mass range of intermediate-mass black holes (IMBHs, $10^{2-6}\,M_\odot$), whose expected variability damping timescales are of order days. We use the high-cadence photometry of the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) to search for optical variability within its individual 28-day sectors. By applying the Quaver light-curve reduction pipeline and designing artifact-aware variability metrics, we identify 3 sources with high-confidence variability and 17 sources with medium-confidence variability. Fitting damped random walk (DRW) models reveals a tentative timescale-mass correlation in a small, metric-selected subset, but not in the full sample. ZTF difference-image aperture photometry reveals long-term fractional variability of 1.8-7.4% in the high-confidence sources, while most medium-confidence sources have variability below 1%. This study demonstrates that the dense cadence and repeated full-sky observations of TESS are valuable for detecting IMBH optical variability, while also being limited by its instrumental artifacts and relatively shallow field.  

Blowing star formation away in AGN Hosts (BAH) - VI. Warm Molecular Outflows Driven by the Low-Luminosity AGN in NGC 3884  
José Henrique Costa-Souza, Rogemar A. Riffel, Nadia L. Zakamska, Marina Bianchin, Maitê S. Z. de Mellos, Rogério Riffel, Gabriel L. Souza-Oliveira, Thaisa Storchi-Bergmann  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27530v1  
Published: 2026-11-20T12:27:12Z  
We use the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) Mid-Infrared Instrument (MIRI) Medium Resolution Spectrograph (MRS) to investigate the warm molecular and low ionization gas outflows driven by the low-luminosity Active Galactic Nucleus (AGN) in NGC 3884. By decomposing the emission-line profiles into multiple Gaussian components, we detect both ionized and warm molecular outflows, traced by [Ar ii] 6.99 um and H2 0-0 S(5), respectively. The ionized and warm molecular components both extend to similar distances from the nucleus, reaching approximately ~1.2 kpc. In addition to an outflow component, we detect a double narrow emission component in both ionized and molecular lines, clearly observed along the minor axis with similar amplitudes, while their intensity ratio varies systematically along the major axis. Both components exhibit similar line widths and centroid velocity distributions, indicating that they are not associated with outflows, but instead arise from gas in the galaxy disk. We estimate that the outflow injects momentum into the interstellar medium (ISM) at a rate corresponding to ~0.015 percent of the AGN bolometric luminosity, consistent with a radiation-driven origin. Assuming a constant outflow rate, the gas reservoir would be depleted on a timescale of ~26 Myr, potentially suppressing nuclear star formation in the host galaxy.  

Accurately Measuring Variability Properties from Noisy Light Curves  
Zhen-Bo Su, Zhen-Yi Cai, Ruisong Xia, Shi-Jiang Chen, Lulu Fan, Hengxiao Guo, Mouyuan Sun, Jun-Xian Wang  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27236v1  
Published: 2026-11-20T07:37:23Z  
Modern time-domain surveys probe optical variability near photometric error, challenging weak-signal recovery. The structure function (SF) is widely used for characterizing variability of active galactic nuclei (AGNs), but its statistical recovery limits are unclear. Using simulated light curves with realistic errors, we compare three SF estimators from the literature: two non-parametric (pairwise noise subtraction and Bayesian) and one parametric. All recover intrinsic SF when variability dominates, but diverge in the weak regime. The pairwise noise-subtraction estimator approaches an artificial floor when negative variance estimates are discarded, with the floor set by photometric precision and sampling rather than intrinsic variability. The parametric method suffers degeneracy, while the Bayesian estimator yields nearly unbiased SF estimates given well-calibrated photometric errors and adequate sampling. We also derive an idealized detection threshold linking intrinsic variability, photometric precision, and pair counts, providing a reference for assessing the detectability of weak signals. Our results establish statistical limits for SF measurements in the weak-variability regime and offer a framework for accurately measuring low-amplitude variability in AGNs (e.g., extremely stable quasars) and in new populations such as little red dots discovered by JWST.  

Disentangling Disk and Broad-Line Region Continuum Emission in a Sample of Active Galactic Nuclei  
Manqi Fu, Shuying Zhou, Hengxiao Guo, Zhi-Xiang Zhang, Marcin Marculewicz, Mouyuan Sun  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27051v1  
Published: 2026-11-20T03:20:49Z  
Recent continuum reverberation-mapping measurements of active galactic nuclei (AGNs) indicate that the inter-band time lags are significantly larger than those predicted by the light travel times across the standard thin model. In addition, the observed $u$-band excess suggests the presence of diffuse continuum (DC) radiation originating in the broad-line region (BLR). Spectroscopically, the Balmer and Paschen jumps in DC emission are unique indicators to quantify the contributions of the BLR to the observed continuum. Here, we study the DC contributions of 31 AGNs, whose spectra are obtained with the Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph (STIS) on the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) and with X-shooter on the Very Large Telescope (VLT). In each spectrum, at least one of the Balmer and Paschen jumps is covered. For each object, we performed radiative transfer calculations using Cloudy within the Locally Optimally-emitting Clouds (LOC) model to construct source-specific DC spectral templates. We quantified the DC contribution by incorporating these templates as continuum components in spectral decomposition. The average ratio of the DC to total (i.e., disk+DC) continua is ~33% just below the Balmer jump (~3625 Å). The Balmer jump provides a stronger constraint on the DC component than the Paschen jump. Simulations based on mock spectra further demonstrate that an unsuitable template can introduce systematic, wavelength-dependent biases to the inferred DC fraction. Our results underscore the importance of employing appropriate DC templates in the spectral decomposition to infer the substantial DC contribution. Upcoming time-resolved spectra will enable more detailed modelling and tighter constraints on the disk and BLR.  

The role of gas on the collisional runaway path to supermassive black hole seeds  
Boris Cuevas, Bastián Reinoso, Andrés Escala, Marcelo C. Vergara, Sebastián Valdebenito, Dominik R. G. Schleicher  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26885v1  
Published: 2026-11-19T22:57:49Z  
The origin of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) remains unclear, despite observational evidence for their rapid formation in the early Universe, as evidenced by active galactic nuclei (AGN) at high redshift. In this paper, we investigate stellar dynamics in dense, gas-embedded clusters. We explore how gas influences the runaway collision process and the subsequent formation of SMBH seeds in a global instability scenario. We perform a series of coupled N-body and SPH simulations using the AMUSE framework, with the stellar and gaseous components interacting gravitationally via the BRIDGE method. We use 12 initial condition models with equal-mass stars following Plummer distributions, with total masses ranging from $10^3$ to $5 \times 10^5~M_{\odot}$, virial radii from $0.005$ to $0.1$ pc, and stellar masses of $1$, $10$ and $50$ $M_{\odot}$. For each model, we perform four simulations with gas fractions ranging from 0\% to 20\%, resulting in a total of 48 simulations. The gas is sampled with the same spatial distribution as the stars, with a mass ratio per particle of $m_{\rm SPH}/m = 0.01$, where $m$ is the stellar mass. We find that in most of our simulations, higher gas fractions lead to the formation of more massive black hole, as the core collapses earlier, triggering collisions through gas--star interactions. Our results show increases in BH mass of $\sim 8\%$ for low gas fractions, to $\sim 44\%$ at 20\% gas fraction. The core collapse time is also shortened, with the strongest reduction at $20\%$ gas fraction ($\sim 27\%$). The transonic regime of our initial conditions is where dynamical friction is most efficient in principle. Although our simulations do not fully resolve this effect, gas--star interactions are the principal mechanism driving the enhancement in BH formation efficiency, in the absence of other dissipation mechanisms.

We present Keck/NIRES, VLT/X-shooter, and Magellan/FIRE spectroscopic follow-up observations of photometrically selected Little Red Dot (LRD) candidates targeting H$α$ at $2&lt;z&lt;3$. The apparent decline of LRDs at cosmic noon raises the question whether they retain the spectra of the high-redshift population. From a parent sample of 266 unresolved sources with V-shaped broadband SEDs, identified in 19.9\,deg$^2$ of the Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program Deep/UltraDeep fields, we observe 24 and confirm broad H$α$ in 6. The remaining 18 candidates show no broad H$α$. Four have Ly$α$ redshifts, which place H$α$ outside our coverage for two. The other 14 have only photometric redshifts, so their H$α$ coverage is uncertain; they may have incorrect photometric redshifts, broad H$α$ fainter than $L_{\mathrm{H}α}\approx3\times10^{42}$ erg s$^{-1}$, or not be LRDs. Archival \textit{JWST}/NIRCam imaging and NIRSpec spectroscopy independently confirm one source as an LRD. One of the six is X-ray detected at a luminosity typical of ordinary AGN and is better described as a broad-line AGN. Only one of seven dust-reddened F2M quasars and two of 38 heavily reddened quasars at $2&lt;z&lt;3$ meet typical JWST V-shape criteria. Four of the six sources with broad H$α$ ($67$\%) resemble JWST-selected LRDs, showing that ground-based photometric selection and spectroscopy can identify LRDs.
