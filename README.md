X-ray Flare Evolution and Changing-Look Nature of the TeV Blazar OQ 334  
Navaneeth P K, Gopal Bhatta, Cosimo Nigro  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21518v1  
Published: 2026-11-16T18:11:49Z  
The blazar OQ 334 (B2 1420+326, z = 0.682) is one of the few jetted AGN with repeated changing-look (CL) transitions between FSRQ and BL Lac states. We present a multi-wavelength study covering 2017 December to 2024 September, focusing on three X-ray flares observed with Swift-XRT (X1: 2019 June 26--July 24, X2: 2020 January 4--February 21, X3: 2024 June 24--August 2) and on the spectral properties of the CL states. The spectrum hardens from flare to flare, with $Γ$ decreasing from $1.97 \pm 0.09$ to $1.71 \pm 0.04$ and $1.34 \pm 0.06$, while the hysteresis evolves from an initial clockwise loop, consistent with cooling-dominated emission, to multi-loop trajectories in the later flares. The X-ray spectra separate the CL states, with FSRQ states hardest ($Γ_X \approx 1.3$--$1.5$), BL Lac states softest ($Γ_X \approx 1.8$--$2.4$), and transition states intermediate. Within the CL states, flux and photon index are positively correlated ($r = 0.59$, $p = 2.5 \times 10^{-2}$), producing a softer-when-brighter trend. The first two flares coincide with FSRQ $\rightarrow$ BL Lac transitions, whereas the third, the brightest, remains FSRQ-like. One-zone leptonic modelling of the broadband SEDs reproduces the flares with a weak, nearly constant magnetic field and a particle-dominated region, and the X-ray emission evolves from a substantial synchrotron contribution in the first flare to complete inverse-Compton dominance in the third. The X-ray band traces the changing balance between the synchrotron and inverse-Compton components in the flares and provides a sensitive probe of the CL behaviour of the source.  

Conditions for mass-loading: Investigating jet-disk material exchange in strongly magnetised accretion flows  
Subhrat Praharaj, M. T. P. Liska, Sera B. Markoff, Koushik Chatterjee, Bart Ripperda, Lorenzo Sironi,  and, Aylecia S. Lattimer  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21401v1  
Published: 2026-11-16T16:53:20Z  
Relativistic jets are produced by accreting black holes across a wide range of masses, from stellar-mass X-ray binaries to supermassive active galactic nuclei (AGN). In low-luminosity AGN, these jets are believed to be powered by strongly magnetised accretion flows, a picture supported by Event Horizon Telescope and VLBI observations of M87. The jets often exhibit high-energy flaring emission which, if partly hadronic, implies that baryons must be loaded into the jet and accelerated despite strong magnetic barriers. However, the mechanism behind this remains unclear. We present high-resolution, three-dimensional General Relativistic Magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations of a high-spin Magnetically Arrested Disk (MAD), a strongly magnetised accretion regime. Using passive Eulerian tracers, we track the exchange of material between the jet and disk to quantify mixing. We find that magnetic flux eruptions, a hallmark of MAD accretion, expel magnetic flux tubes initially filled with jet material (expected to be pair-dominated in real systems). Through magnetic reconnection during these eruptions, this material is mixed into the disk. Crucially, the same flux tubes reconnect the disk back to the jet, allowing disk material, expected to be primarily hadronic, to infiltrate the jet sheath and boundary. This disk-to-jet injection offers a natural mass-loading mechanism that does not require the jet to be globally baryon-dominated. The resulting ejection-injection cycle may instead drive episodic hadronic acceleration, providing a viable pathway for high-energy emission in relativistic jets.

Impact of the Initial Lorentz Factor on Kinetic Instabilities, Magnetic-Field Evolution, and Particle Acceleration in Relativistic Jets  
Ram Chandra Pageni, Kenichi Nishikawa, Athina Meli, Ioana Dutan, Jacob There Frederiksen, Christoph Koehn, Nicholas R. MacDonald, Yosuke Mizuno  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21109v1  
Published: 2026-11-16T13:58:01Z  
We investigate how the initial Lorentz factor shapes the evolution of relativistic jets---a key open question in high-energy astrophysics. Using global three-dimensional particle-in-cell (PIC) simulations with cylindrical jet geometry on a Cartesian grid, we analyze the self-consistent evolution of relativistic electron--positron and electron--ion jets from active galactic nuclei (AGN) and their interaction with the ambient medium. We implement an injection scheme that assigns space- and time-dependent velocities to injected particles and simulate jets with initial Lorentz factors of $γ_{0} = 10$ and 15. Jets with the higher Lorentz factor ($γ_{0} = 15$) exhibit stronger electron collimation, whereas those with $γ_{0} = 10$ show larger growth rates of kinetic instabilities during the linear stage. In both cases, a non-oscillatory electric field accelerates and decelerates charged particles throughout the linear and nonlinear stages. Upon entering the nonlinear regime, the initial toroidal magnetic field undergoes significant amplification driven by the Weibel instability (WI), the kinetic Kelvin--Helmholtz instability (kKHI), and the mushroom instability (MI), and subsequently reorganizes into complex topologies. In the late nonlinear stage, magnetic islands with randomly swirling fields indicate a highly turbulent magnetic structure. These findings demonstrate that global 3D~PIC modeling is essential for capturing the interplay among kinetic instabilities, magnetic turbulence, and particle acceleration in relativistic outflows, bridging microscopic plasma physics with macroscopic observations of AGN jets and gamma-ray bursts.  

The imprint of neutron escape on non-thermal protons in AGN coronae  
Antonio Ambrosone, Damiano F. G. Fiorillo  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21047v1  
Published: 2026-11-16T12:59:46Z  
The coronae of active galactic nuclei (AGN) are promising sites of proton acceleration to hundreds of TeV, potentially accounting for the neutrino excess from NGC 1068 reported by IceCube. We show that photohadronic proton-to-neutron conversion provides an important proton escape channel, as neutrons can leave the corona well before decaying. In magnetized turbulent coronae, neutron escape can reduce the characteristic neutrino energy by up to a factor of four and the integrated neutrino luminosity by approximately 30% at a fixed non-thermal proton energy density. More strikingly, proton-to-neutron conversion offers an escape route roughly two orders of magnitude faster than proton diffusion, requiring a correspondingly larger proton injection rate to sustain the non-thermal population. The effect is considerably weaker when proton escape is already ballistic, as expected in magnetic-reconnection scenarios. Therefore, neutron escape is essential for quantitatively connecting neutrino emission from AGN coronae to the efficiency of proton injection into the acceleration process.

The mass density slope of early-type galaxies: newest results from the redshift distribution of strong gravitational lenses  
Xinyue Jiang, Shuo Cao, Wuzheng Guo, Chenfa Zheng, Chengsheng Mu, Shuaibo Geng, Marek Biesiada, Yalong Nan  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20141v1  
Published: 2026-11-15T17:16:26Z  
The mass distribution of early-type galaxies (ETGs) encodes key information about baryonic processes and dark-matter assembly. In this paper, we propose a new framework to investigate the mass-density profiles of ETGs and their evolution with redshift, based on the redshift distribution of strong gravitational lenses. We compile 200 galaxy-scale strong lensing systems with complete information, including lens and source redshifts, Einstein radii, and spectroscopic velocity dispersions. To account for the heterogeneous construction of the compiled sample, we model a lens-redshift-dependent selection function for each survey category. By combining the lens-redshift distribution with velocity dispersion function (VDF) and spherical Jeans-based mapping, we obtain population-level constraints on the total mass-density slope gamma in three lens-redshift intervals, marginalizing over the luminosity-density slope delta and the stellar orbital anisotropy beta. We find that the total mass density of ETGs remains close to isothermal at low and intermediate redshifts ($γ=2.00^{+0.07}_{-0.15}$ at 0 &lt; z_l &lt; 0.5 and $γ=1.96^{+0.16}_{-0.26}$ at 0.5 &lt; z_l &lt; 0.6), with a shallower effective slope at higher redshift ($γ=1.81^{+0.17}_{-0.21}$ at $0.6&lt;z_l\lesssim1.0$). The marginalized posteriors of delta and beta remain broad, consistent with $δ\simeq2$ and with isotropic orbits in all three redshift intervals. These results are consistent with the redshift trend predicted by Horizon-AGN simulations, in which active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback, together with stellar assembly and merging, contributes to the structural evolution of ETGs. Applied to the much larger lens samples expected from future missions such as the Chinese Space Station Telescope (CSST), this method will enable tighter tests of density-slope evolution and its connection to galaxy formation processes.
