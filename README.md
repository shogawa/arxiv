A search and multi-frequency study of curved parsec-scale jets in active galaxies  
V. A. Makeev, A. B. Pushkarev, Y. Y. Kovalev  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34496v1  
Published: 2026-11-25T14:53:41Z  
Very long baseline interferometry (VLBI) observations show that a fraction of active galactic nucleus (AGN) jets exhibit pronounced apparent bending on parsec scales, potentially strongly enhanced by projection effects. While individual sources have been studied in detail, the statistical properties of curved jets and the physical mechanisms responsible for their morphology remain poorly constrained. We aim to select a statistically robust sample of AGNs with significantly curved parsec-scale jets and to constrain the physical mechanisms responsible for jet bending. We analyse 123 456 VLBI images of 17 478 AGNs collected by the public Astrogeo database. After filtering for extended structure, we extract and stack multi-epoch, multi-frequency (1.2--86 GHz) ridge lines. We fit linear and logarithmic helices representing jet precession and resonant Kelvin--Helmholtz instability mode. We combine model-fits and Monte Carlo simulations to further constrain these physical models. We robustly identify 586 AGNs (7.4% of sources with resolved jets) with curved parsec-scale jets. Compared to straight jets, curved ones are less common at low redshifts, possibly due to the sample-related selection effects. In the precession scenario, the inferred periods span $P \sim$10--10 000 yr, with a logarithmic mean of $\sim 220$ yr. Solid-body disc precession in supermassive black hole binaries is broadly consistent with these periods, whereas geodetic precession requires extremely tight, near-equal-mass systems. Lense--Thirring precession remains viable for both thin and thick discs. Kelvin--Helmholtz instability also provides a potential explanation. However, identifying the preferred model for each individual source requires further investigation, including temporal analysis and jet kinematics.  

A Variability Metric Pipeline for AGN studies  
Diego Martínez Collipal, Swayamtrupta Panda  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33435v1  
Published: 2026-11-24T21:02:30Z  
Variability in active galactic nuclei (AGNs) is remarkably diverse, spanning multiple timescales, wavelengths, and physical processes. Variability probes accretion physics and the interplay between supermassive black holes and their surroundings. Building on early case studies, the field has shifted toward population-level analyses of increasingly large datasets, making scalable techniques crucial for upcoming time-domain surveys. We developed a Python-based pipeline to analyze AGN light curves using variability metrics and applied it to 14,061 nearby AGNs ($z\lesssim 0.09$) observed with ZTF. We examined metric distributions and correlations. Amplitude-based metrics (e.g., $F_{\rm var}$) help identify major flares but are sensitive to single deviant epochs. Temporal metrics such as $η$ provide a complementary check. Comparison with WISE infrared photometry suggests that $η$ traces the strength of AGN activity. Most Type~I and Type~II AGNs in our sample exhibit similar variability patterns based on these metrics. This overlap may partly reflect misclassifications that targeted spectroscopic follow-up could help resolve. To identify subsets of AGNs with distinctive variability, we used UMAP to group sources with similar metric values. The resulting embedding shows a region enriched in high-amplitude variables, including a subset with long-term flux trends. We find evidence that extremely variable quasars and changing-look AGNs may populate this region, which can help prioritize sources for follow-up. Our pipeline offers a scalable, survey-agnostic framework suitable for application to Vera C. Rubin Observatory's LSST and other ongoing and upcoming photometric surveys.
