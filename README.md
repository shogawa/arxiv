Excitation and Destruction of Small Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons by the Luminous Seyfert Nucleus of NGC 4151  
Grant P. Donnelly, J. -D. T. Smith, Sebastian F. Hönig, Daniel A. Dale, Tanio Díaz-Santos, B. T. Draine, Brandon S. Hensley, Thomas S. -Y. Lai, Karin M. Sandstrom, Andrey Vayner  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36651v1  
Published: 2026-10-27T05:10:44Z  
We present a spatially-resolved detection of $3.3\,\mathrm{μm}$ emission from polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) within the circumnuclear ${\sim}200\,\mathrm{pc}$ of the archetypal Seyfert galaxy NGC 4151, using new JWST NIRSpec/IFU spectroscopy. PAHs are detected down to the innermost $50\,\mathrm{pc}$, though it is likely that PAHs also exist at smaller radii, as PAH detections closer than this seem to be limited by continuum dilution. This is contrary to expectations that PAH grains, especially the smallest grains that carry the $3.3\,\mathrm{μm}$ feature, could not survive near an active galactic nucleus (AGN). Furthermore, the surface brightness of PAH emission increases as radius from the nucleus decreases. We interpret this as evidence that the AGN supplies energy for PAH excitation by driving ultraviolet emission in nearby H and $\mathrm{H_2}$ gas. We do not detect PAH emission within the narrow line region (NLR) that hosts highly ionized and outflowing gas, suggesting that the AGN may also destroy PAHs in these regions, depending on geometry. Together, these results show that an AGN has a complex, multifaceted role in the life cycle of PAHs and in PAH excitation.  

ASKAP Discovery of a Giant Odd Radio Circle at $z=0.15$  
Renzhi Su, Minfeng Gu  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36512v1  
Published: 2026-10-27T02:16:59Z  
Odd radio circles (ORCs) are a recently recognized class of faint, roughly circular diffuse radio sources, typically a few hundred kiloparsecs across, whose origin is still debated. We report the serendipitous discovery of a giant ORC, ORC~J1928$-$5056, in 856~MHz continuum data from the ASKAP observations. The emission forms a single, mildly elliptical structure $4^{\prime}$ across, corresponding to $0.63$~Mpc at the redshift $z=0.1507$ of the central galaxy LEDA~140850, the brightest cluster galaxy of Abell~3639, which makes it one of the largest single ORCs known. After subtracting the radio nucleus associated with the host galaxy, the ORC has a flux density of 73~mJy integrated within an area of $\sim 12$~arcmin$^2$, i.e.\ a mean surface brightness of only $1.7$~$μ$Jy~arcsec$^{-2}$, and a rest-frame luminosity of $L_{1.4}=2\times10^{24}$~W~Hz$^{-1}$, comparable to those of the most luminous ORCs. Including its nucleus, the system reaches $L_{1.4}=6.6\times10^{24}$~W~Hz$^{-1}$, higher than that of any other ORC reported so far. The host is a massive ($M_{\ast}\sim10^{11}\,M_{\odot}$), passive elliptical galaxy with negligible star formation. It contains an AGN jet with a power-law spectrum of index $α=-0.877\pm0.089$ between 856 and 1656~MHz. Finally, we discuss the implications for ORC formation models and the observations needed to establish the nature of this system.

E. Järvelä, P. Romano, J. Nättilä, J. D. Paul, A. Lähteenmäki, M. Berton, T. Savolainen, S. Panda, A. C. S. Readhead, M. Tornikoski

Recently, a sample of radio-weak active galactic nuclei (AGN) was discovered to exhibit extreme Jy-level radio flares at 37~GHz. The origin of these extraordinary flares remains unknown. All sources host low-mass supermassive black holes, and most of them can be classified as narrow-line Seyfert 1 galaxies. We employed multiwavelength observations to investigate one of these sources, SDSS J122844.81+501751.2, during a period of high 37~GHz activity. Our data include radio observations from Metsähovi Radio Observatory (MRO) at 37~GHz, OVRO at 15~GHz, and JVLA at 15, 22, and 33~GHz, as well as optical, UV, and X-ray observations from \textit{Swift}/XRT and UVOT. We studied the variability of the multiwavelength light curves (LC) using statistical tests, as well as derived the $e$-folding timescales of the flares. Utilising the timescales and the observed flare properties, we estimated the variability brightness temperatures and Doppler factors associated with the flares. Most individual LCs do not show statistically significant variability, which is likely due to the low number of measurements. The shortest timescale we estimated is only 1.1~hours, measured between MRO 37~GHz and JVLA 33~GHz detections. This is the fastest intrinsic radio variability reported in an AGN so far. This yielded an extreme variability brightness temperature of $T_\mathrm{B}$ = 4.20 $~\times$ 10$^{18}$~K and a variability Doppler factor of 438. We discuss magnetic eruptions near the black hole as a possible explanation for the extreme radio flares, and show that both incoherent and coherent processes can produce flares at these timescales. These flares challenge our current understanding of AGN radio variability. Whereas magnetic eruptions offer a viable explanation for the flares, more observations, preferably broadband and including polarisation, will be required to confirm their origin. (abridged)  

Unveiling the nonthermal environments of NGC 3411 and NGC 5903 through new MeerKAT observations  
T. Pasini, F. De Gasperin, E. O'Sullivan, M. Brüggen, M. Gaspari, K. Kolokythas, E. Bulbul, E. Bahar  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36170v1  
Published: 2026-10-26T19:03:38Z  
Galaxy groups are key laboratories for investigating the interplay between active galactic nucleus (AGN) outbursts and the dynamical processes of the intra-group medium. We aim to characterize the morphology, spectral properties, and magnetic field structure of the diffuse nonthermal radio emission in two peculiar galaxy groups, NGC 3411 and NGC 5903, selected from an originally larger sample, in order to constrain its origin and its connection with the dynamics of the intra-group medium. We present new MeerKAT UHF- and L-band observations of the two groups, complemented by archival Chandra and XMM-Newton data already analyzed in previous works. In NGC 3411, we detect extended radio emission surrounding the central AGN over scales of ~70 kpc, and resolve previously undetected arc-shaped substructures on scales of ~20-30 kpc. This extended component exhibits a relatively uniform steep spectrum ($α$ ~ -1.7). We discuss possible scenarios for its origin, including mini-halos, aged AGN plasma, weak shocks, and the possibility of a radio galaxy observed along the line of sight. In NGC 5903, MeerKAT reveals a complex filamentary morphology, including a previously unknown ring-like structure surrounding the central AGN. Spectral-index mapping shows localized flattening ($α$ ~ -0.9) along the north arc, while polarization data reveal ordered magnetic fields and a patchy rotation measure distribution across the source. Together with the disturbed X-ray morphology, these results support a scenario in which fossil AGN plasma has been redistributed and locally reenergized by group-scale dynamical processes such as turbulence, shear, and weak compression. Our results demonstrate that galaxy groups can host a rich variety of nonthermal phenomena, whose properties can only be fully revealed through the combination of sensitive, high-resolution, and multifrequency radio observations.  

Heavy Seeds and the Multi-scale Fueling of Early Supermassive Black Holes and Little Red Dots  
Junehyoung Jeon, Volker Bromm, Boyuan Liu, Stella S. R. Offner, Anthony J. Taylor, Steven L. Finkelstein  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36089v1  
Published: 2026-10-26T18:13:38Z  
The formation of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) in the early Universe has been a longstanding mystery due to the brief period available to assemble them. More recently, the unexpected discovery with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) of Little Red Dots (LRDs), compact sources exhibiting signatures of active galactic nuclei (AGN), has accentuated this challenge. Cosmological simulations investigating their formation and evolution confront the limitation that the compact scale of SMBH/LRD physics cannot be simultaneously resolved with the larger-scale cosmological environment. We address this limitation by utilizing a cutout technique, selecting a targeted region around an SMBH seed from a cosmological parent simulation and re-running the cutout at higher (sub-pc) resolution for shorter periods. We explore two SMBH formation scenarios: the standard, light seed stellar-remnant case, and the heavy-seed, direct collapse black hole (DCBH) channel. Within the high-resolution achieved by the cutouts, the DCBH, due to its larger mass, is able to induce a buildup of high density gas nearby and accrete gas in bursts of super-Eddington phases, growing in mass and producing an LRD-like spectrum. In contrast, the stellar remnant cannot do so and remains dormant, unable to affect the nearby gas or significantly evolve by gas accretion alone. Therefore, we find that observed high-redshift SMBHs and LRDs require the existence of heavy seeds to assemble dense gas around themselves, enabling efficient growth and producing red optical spectra with strong Balmer breaks resembling LRDs.
