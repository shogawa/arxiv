KCWI Observations of Type 2 Quasar-driven Ionized Outflows at z~0.1  
Alison L. Coil, Saloni Agrawal, David S. N. Rupke, Serena Perrotta, Natalie Lam, Wenmeng Ning, Casey Carlile, Yu-Hsuan Teng  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08828v1  
Published: 2026-11-06T00:32:48Z  
AGN-driven outflows play a crucial role in regulating star formation in galaxies. These outflows are known to be multi-phase, and the characterization of the kinematics, extent, and energetics of each gas phase is needed to obtain a full picture of the outflow properties and impact. This study presents Keck Cosmic Web Imager (KCWI) integral field spectroscopy of the ionized gas outflow properties in seven low redshift (z&lt;0.1) quasars---six Type 2 AGN and one narrow-line Type 1 AGN---providing insight into the warm ionized outflows in these luminous AGN. With the large field-of-view and high sensitivity of KCWI, these data reveal both larger ionized outflow extents than were previously known for these sources and uncover the existence of additional high-velocity kinematic components in the ionized outflows of these luminous AGN. We find (a) a high incidence (50-70%) of multiple outflows on scales of 4--10 kpc and (b) trends between the outflow extent, kinematics, and the ionized mass and energy outflow rates. These trends imply that the faster and often smaller outflows carry higher mass and energy outflow rates than the slower, larger outflows. We also find that the ionized gas outflows reach as far as the known extent of narrow-line ionized gas emission in AGN, which is known to depend weakly on AGN luminosity. This work highlights the utility of the current generation of wide-field integral field spectrographs on 8--10m class telescopes for studying quasar-driven outflows.  

Magnetically Arrested Accretion and Black Hole Spin in a Semi-Analytic Model of Galaxy Formation  
Antonio J. Porras-Valverde, Angelo Ricarte, Priyamvada Natarajan, Hyerin Cho, Ramesh Narayan, Kung-Yi Su, Cora Prather  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08599v1  
Published: 2026-11-05T19:00:10Z  
We present {\sc Mad Sage}, a semi-analytic model of galaxy formation that couples black hole mass and spin evolution to gas accretion and AGN feedback under magnetically arrested disk conditions. Building on the {\sc Dark Sage} model, we incorporate GRMHD-calibrated prescriptions for spin evolution and jet power, spin-dependent radiative efficiencies, and remnant spins from BH-BH mergers. We compare accretion and feedback models, including coherent disk-fed accretion with mixed coherent and incoherent hot-halo accretion, with and without additional mechanical feedback. We find that incoherent accretion, together with Blandford-Znajek spin-down, drives massive BHs toward low spins, reducing jet efficiencies and weakening the quenching of massive galaxies. Reproducing the high-mass end of the stellar mass function requires either exclusively coherent accretion or an additional feedback component provided by ADAF outflows. Our models broadly reproduce observed BH-galaxy scaling relations, disk surface density profiles, and the AGN luminosity function at $z\sim 2-6$, although the spin models under-predict the number density of faint AGN at low redshift. At $z\lesssim 3$, predicted BH spins decrease with increasing BH mass, while at higher redshifts, the mass dependence weakens. Spin distributions peak at $|a_\star|\sim 0.5$-$0.7$, below current X-ray reflection spectroscopy measurements, and become bimodal for massive, quiescent BHs accessible with the next generation Event Horizon Telescope and the Black Hole EXplorer. We report that the predicted LISA event rates are nearly independent of the adopted accretion and spin models.
