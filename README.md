The incidence of radio-loud active galactic nuclei (RLAGN) and their energetic output via AGN feedback are crucial in understanding the evolution of galaxies and the large-scale structure of the Universe. In the local Universe, the incidence of RLAGN has been shown to correlate strongly with host galaxy properties, particularly stellar mass. Using the large-area survey capabilities provided by Euclid and the LOw Frequency ARray (LOFAR), we present the first study of the RLAGN incidence--stellar mass relation to $z=3.0$. We perform a stringent analysis accounting for cross-matching reliability and stellar-mass incompleteness. In addition, we test the robustness of our results against AGN and host galaxy classification. This yields a sample of $3\,154$ RLAGN in the Euclid Deep Field North, comprising $2\,803$ low-excitation radio galaxies (LERGs). We find that the LERG incidence as a function of stellar mass and radio luminosity follows a tight correlation over more than two orders of magnitude in stellar mass to $z=3.0$. The slope of the power-law relation decreases from $γ\approx2.5$ in the local Universe to $γ\approx1.0$ at $z=3.0$, indicating a relative increase in low-mass LERGs at earlier epochs. Separating the LERG population into actively star-forming and quiescent galaxies reveals that quiescent LERGs show a much steeper relation between incidence and stellar mass, with $γ\approx2.5$ compared to star-forming LERGs that show $γ\approx1.5$. However, the relation seems to be independent of whether galaxies are star-forming or quiescent at $z\geq2$, where both populations show relatively flat relations with $γ\approx1.0$. Our results suggest an evolutionary picture in which the LERG population transitions from cold-mode, merger-dominated accretion at earlier epochs to a scenario in which systems require a massive hot halo to fuel the AGN in the local Universe.

Active galactic nucleus (AGN) variability is widely recognized as a promising avenue for constraining black hole mass, yet its practical application remains limited. Although the correlation between black hole mass and variability timescale has been extensively investigated, finite observational baselines often lead to systematic underestimation of the timescale, thereby hindering reliable mass estimation for individual sources. To address this limitation, we develop the Black hole ADaptive Multi-relation Mass Estimation based on Timing analysis (BADMET), an extendable framework that provides black hole mass estimates by assimilating multiple mass-related parameters that include variability properties. The discovery with JWST of accreting black holes (BHs) at z=4-9 in numbers far exceeding pre-JWST expectations, many of them overmassive relative to their hosts and linked to the enigmatic Little Red Dots (LRDs), raises the question of how early BHs form and grow. Confirming and characterising about 100 pairs requires of order 1000 hr, well beyond the ceiling of standard TAC programs, and demands multi-cycle continuity. The census will deliver pair fractions as functions of redshift, separation and luminosity, BH masses and mass ratios, host properties and environments, and the empirical anchor needed to turn merger models into realistic LISA rate forecasts.  

Lord/Egg of the LRDs: One geometry for its many faces  
Piero Madau, Roberto Maiolino, Xihan Ji, Xiaojing Lin, Xiaohui Fan  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22316v1  
Published: 2026-11-17T07:14:04Z  
Little red dots (LRDs) combine V-shaped ultraviolet-optical continua, broad Balmer emission, and weak X-rays. The nearby analog SDSS J102530.29+140207.3, known as Lord/Egg, offers detailed spectroscopy and polarimetry. We test one active galactic nucleus (AGN) geometry by fitting its ultraviolet-to-infrared continuum with an inclination-dependent accretion-disk spectral energy distribution (SED), young stars, and a clumpy dusty screen. The same SED is used in Cloudy calculations of a radially stratified, virialized broad-line region (BLR). The fit favors log10(M_BH/M_sun) about 7.4, highly super-Eddington accretion, inclination above 70 degrees, and line-of-sight cloud extinction of about 3 mag. It also predicts a 2 keV luminosity below the Chandra limit. A BLR whose density rises from 10^9 to 10^11 cm^-3 near the sublimation radius reproduces the broad H-alpha profile without a separate intermediate-width Gaussian. For cloud columns near 10^23 cm^-2, it also matches the different Balmer-line widths, exponential-like wings, and Balmer decrement without electron scattering. The redshifted H-beta-H-delta absorber lacks an H-alpha counterpart, requiring velocity-coherent H-alpha re-emission into the absorption trough. Cool outer clumps explain K I and saturated Na I absorption, while the geometry recovers the continuum and H-alpha polarization and their position-angle offset. Ca II triplet absorption requires localized, non-radiatively heated, warm dust-poor zones near the sublimation front. He I absorption traces a fast, about 500 km s^-1, low-column ionized BLR skin. All projected absorber velocities remain below the local escape speeds, consistent with a bound failed BLR wind. Thus, a dusty, high-inclination accretion system can explain Lord/Egg within one connected geometry.

URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22044v1  
Published: 2026-11-17T01:10:23Z  
The coupling between AGN feedback and the multiphase circumgalactic medium (CGM) is central to understanding galaxy evolution. In a previous paper, we showed that the black hole mass ($M_{\rm BH}$) correlates negatively with cold gas mass in IllustrisTNG100. We now extend this to the Eddington ratio ($λ_{\rm Edd}$), which traces the current accretion state, using the same sample of 5089 central galaxies at $z=0$ under the same group-specific partial correlation framework, controlling for stellar and dark matter halo mass. We find that $λ_{\rm Edd}$ correlates positively with warm gas in star-forming galaxies ($ρ\approx 0.6$-$0.7$), persisting after jointly controlling for $M_{\rm BH}$, the mass in the other non-star-forming gas phases, and specific star formation rate. The $λ_{\rm Edd}$-cold gas correlation instead reverses sign, from negative in star-forming galaxies ($ρ\approx -0.32$) to positive in quenched galaxies ($ρ\approx 0.40$). Controlling for $M_{\rm BH}$ leaves this correlation essentially unchanged in all three galaxy types. Further controlling for the total gas mass in the other phases strengthens the star-forming correlation and weakens the quenched correlation to $ρ\approx 0.21$, which remains significant. These results establish the Eddington ratio as a phase- and type-dependent tracer of the gas-black hole connection: the cold gas reservoir records the integrated black hole growth, the warm gas in star-forming galaxies carries the signature of the current accretion state, and the sign reversal of the cold-gas correlation marks the transition between the star-forming and quenched regimes.

Published: 2026-11-16T19:00:05Z  
Baryonic feedback is a major theoretical uncertainty for Stage-IV weak lensing (WL) surveys. The thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) effect traces the hot gas redistributed by feedback, so combining it with WL can calibrate feedback and improve cosmological constraints. Doing so with simulation-based inference requires correlated lensing and Compton-$y$ maps across many cosmologies and feedback scenarios. We present two methods to generate Compton-$y$ maps from dark-matter-only $N$-body light cones using the component-wise baryonification (BFC) model, which produces separate gas and stellar fields and computes the gas pressure from hydrostatic equilibrium. The two methods, painting pressure profiles onto halos or redistributing the signal along the baryonified gas, agree to within a few per cent. We calibrate the model to the fiducial and strongest AGN feedback FLAMINGO simulations by jointly fitting gas mass fractions and thermal pressure profiles, measuring the non-thermal pressure from the simulated gas velocities rather than treating it as a free parameter. The predicted Compton-$y$ profiles match FLAMINGO within halo-to-halo scatter, and when painted onto the FLAMINGO halos we reproduce the tSZ power spectrum of both feedback models to $\sim5\%$ for $500\lesssim\ell\lesssim3000$. Applied to a \emph{CosmoGridV1-like} $N$-body simulation, our maps agree with FLAMINGO within $\sim10\%$ for $300\lesssim\ell\lesssim8000$; the remaining differences come from the few hundred most massive clusters, and removing them from both halo catalogs brings the two into agreement. Our method thus enables accurate and efficient tSZ forward models from $N$-body simulation suites such as CosmoGridV1, an important step towards joint WL and tSZ simulation-based inference for Euclid, LSST, and the Simons Observatory.  

A PhEW Good Simulations: I. Galaxy Properties  
Lisiyuan Yang, Neal Katz, Evan Scannapieco, Aditi Vijayan  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21716v1  
Published: 2026-11-16T19:00:01Z  
Although galactic outflows driven by stellar feedback are widely implemented in modern cosmological simulations, the interactions between them and the circumgalactic medium (CGM) remains poorly resolved. To overcome this limitation, we conduct a suite of cosmological simulations that use the PhEW (Physically Evolved Winds) subgrid model, which analytically tracks the exchange of mass, metals, momentum, and energy by winds with the ambient CGM using an algorithm based on high-resolution cloud-crushing simulations. Our results show that even without AGN feedback, our simulations are able to accurately reproduce the observed galactic stellar mass functions (GSMFs) and stellar mass-halo mass (SMHM) relations at $z\gtrsim 1$, after which the massive galaxies in our simulations start to overproduce stars. Our simulations also match the observed relations of $M_{\mathrm{HI}}-M_*$, $M_{\mathrm{H_2}}-M_*$, $Z_*-M_*$ and $Z_{\mathrm{ISM}}-M_*$. Taken together these results suggest that AGN feedback is much less important for galaxy formation and evolution than previously believed. However, like found in many previous simulations, the specific star formation rates (sSFRs) of our galaxies are significantly lower than those observed at $z\gtrsim1$ at all stellar masses. Significantly, we find that our results are robust against changes in the resolution and volume of our simulations, without the need to retune our wind parameters.
