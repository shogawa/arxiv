Sungho Jung, Jong-Hak Woo  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29173v1  
Published: 2026-10-21T04:04:19Z  
We present a Bayesian framework, Two-stage Inference for Light curve DEcomposition (TILDE), for mapping the accretion disk and the broad line region (BLR) from active galactic nuclei (AGN) multi-band light curves. By inferring delayed BLR contamination from the underlying disk variability, TILDE provides the delay measurements of the disk as well as the BLR. We validate TILDE using synthetic data, finding that TILDE recovers the known time delays of the disk and BLR. By applying TILDE to the Seoul National University AGN Monitoring Project sample, we find that the inferred BLR light curves match the observed H$β$ emission light curves, providing consistent BLR lags compared to the reported H$β$ lags. Using the lag spectrum, effectively corrected for the BLR contamination, we find that the disk size is broadly consistent with the standard thin-disk model, suggesting that BLR contamination can significantly bias the disk size derived from the cross-correlation analysis of the broad-band photometry. These results demonstrate that the new inference method is a powerful tool for determining both disk and BLR sizes from broad-band photometry in the era of time-domain surveys.

We investigate spatial variations in polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) properties and photoelectric heating efficiency in 67 nearby luminous infrared galaxies from the Great Observatories All-sky LIRG Survey (GOALS). Using mid-infrared spectroscopy from Spitzer and far-infrared cooling-line maps from Herschel, we extract spectra in concentric annuli and matched aperture grids out to about 14 kpc. PAH features are measured with the CAFE spectral decomposition tool. In starburst galaxies, the PAH 11.3/7.7 ratio rises out to about 5 kpc while 6.2/7.7 remains nearly constant, indicating predominantly large, neutral PAHs. In starbursts, higher efficiency is associated with more neutral PAHs, while AGN-dominated systems show the opposite relation. These results provide quantitative, spatially resolved constraints on the extent of AGN effects on dust properties in a population of luminous infrared galaxies.

We investigate how star formation and active galactic nuclei (AGNs) shape the ionized and neutral interstellar medium in 67 nearby luminous and ultraluminous infrared galaxies from the Great Observatories All-sky LIRG Survey (GOALS). Combining mid-infrared (MIR) spectral mapping from the Spitzer Infrared Spectrograph with far-infrared (FIR) spectroscopy from the Herschel Photodetector Array Camera and Spectrometer, we measure fine-structure emission lines in aperture grids extending to projected galactocentric distances of about 14 kpc. How does a galaxy's quenching probability depend on its black hole's mass?  
Antonio J. Porras-Valverde, John C. Forbes  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27061v1  
Published: 2026-10-19T18:00:00Z  
We develop a parametric toy model to explore how black hole mass scatter $σ_{\rm BH}$ and the dependence of the quenching probability per unit time on black hole mass, parameterized as a power law with index $q$, jointly control the faint-end slope $α$ of the stellar mass function of quenched central galaxies. By varying $σ_{\rm BH}$ and $q$, we identify two regimes. At low scatter, $σ_{\rm BH} \lesssim 0.4$ dex, $α$ is strongly sensitive to $q$. Gradual quenching, represented by low $q$, produces shallow slopes, while sharp thresholds, with high $q$, produce steep mass functions, making $α$ a powerful diagnostic of AGN feedback prescriptions. At large scatter, $σ_{\rm BH} \gtrsim 0.6$ dex, models with vastly different $q$ converge to similar quenched mass functions, because the intrinsic scatter erases the imprint of the feedback implementation. Comparing to cosmological hydrodynamical simulations and semianalytic models, only models with $σ_{\rm BH} \gtrsim 0.4$ dex match the observed faint-end slope of $α\approx 0.7$. {\sc SAGE}, {\sc Dark Sage}, and {\sc COLIBRE}, with $σ_{\rm BH}$ of 2.0, 0.6, and 0.4 dex, respectively, reproduce this slope, whereas {\sc IllustrisTNG} and {\sc EAGLE}, with $σ_{\rm BH} \lesssim 0.2$ dex, produce steeper slopes. Current observational estimates of $σ_{\rm BH}$ allow a wide range of $q \gtrsim 0.8$, so the observed slope alone cannot identify the quenching prescription. If $σ_{\rm BH}$ is small, simultaneous measurements of $α$ and $σ_{\rm BH}$ can distinguish between competing AGN feedback models.
