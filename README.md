Every galaxy is a radio source: a stellar-mass dependent minimum 150 MHz luminosity identified in the LoTSS Deep Fields  
Shravya Shenoy, Daniel J. B. Smith, Martin J. Hardcastle, Soumyadeep Das, Marina I. Arnaudova, Kenneth J. Duncan, Luke R. Holden, Huub J. A. Röttgering  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26618v1  
Published: 2026-11-19T18:58:31Z  
The second data release of the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey (LoTSS) Deep Fields includes the deepest wide-field 150 MHz mosaics in existence, covering the ELAIS-N1 field. Combining these radio observations with star-formation rate (SFR) and stellar mass estimates for 113,228 near-infrared selected galaxies derived using Prospector, we re-examine the relationship between galaxies' 150 MHz luminosities ($L_{150\rm{MHz}}$), SFRs and stellar masses. We not only recover the established radio luminosity-SFR relation and its dependence on stellar mass, but also discover a mass-dependent minimum radio luminosity that is independent of SFR. This behaviour is especially evident in galaxies with low specific SFR (sSFR $\equiv$ SFR/$M_*$), at $L_{150\rm MHz} \sim 10^{20}-10^{22}$ W Hz$^{-1}$, where star-forming galaxies are expected to dominate. The non-stellar emission is consistent with low-level accretion onto supermassive black holes (i.e. weak active galactic nuclei, AGN), spanning effective Eddington-scaled accretion rates $λ_{\rm Edd} \sim 10^{-5}-10^{-4}$, suggesting that radio emission due to accretion is widespread and effectively continuous. A two-component model combining star formation- and accretion-driven emission reproduces the observed trends, demonstrating that AGN activity can bias radio-based SFR estimates. The low radio luminosities probed in this work provide a first glimpse of the populations that will be routinely accessible with future facilities such as LOFAR 2.0 and the Square Kilometre Array.

Little Red Dots (LRDs) are compact, red sources discovered by JWST. Most show broad permitted emission lines characteristic of AGN, but other properties differ from local AGN, such as X-ray and radio weakness and an apparent scarcity of dust. If LRDs at z&gt;4 are indeed dust deficient, the bolometric luminosities and black hole (BH) masses measured from SED fitting would be overestimated, and their red optical continuum would be intrinsic rather than reddened. Here we use rest-frame 3-27 micron JWST spectra of a sample of nearby (D&lt;60 Mpc) Seyfert 1 (Sy1) nuclei to place constraints on the dust emission of LRDs. We normalised the individual Sy1 spectra by their broad H$α$ and 5100 A continuum luminosities and stacked them, obtaining scalings that reproduce, within a factor of three, the rest-frame near-infrared emission of a NIRSpec+MIRI stack of 249 LRDs at 2.3&lt;z&lt;9.3 and the near- and mid-infrared emission of a local LRD at z=0.1, with the 5100 A continuum giving the more reliable prediction when modest extinction is considered (Av&lt;1 mag). However, simultaneously reproducing the optical and infrared SEDs of LRDs requires a more reddened accretion disc (Av ~ 2 mag) and a torus with a lower reprocessing efficiency (Ct ~ 0.1-0.2) than inferred for local Sy1 galaxies. This combination offers an alternative to BH-star scenarios that explain the optical continuum as an unreddened blackbody of T ~ 5000 K. Our model, based on local Sy1s, yields a more plausible infrared-to-optical energy balance, suggesting that LRDs are not dust deficient. From the torus model that reproduces the optical and near-infrared SED of the high-redshift LRD stack, we estimate a dust mass of $M_{\rm dust}\simeq10^4\,M_\odot$, consistent with the dust masses typical of AGN tori.  

Disentangling star formation rate indicators in H-ATLAS galaxies: UV, FIR and radio diagnostics from energy-balance SED fitting  
Boitumelo Gaolape, Rhodri Evans  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26260v1  
Published: 2026-11-19T15:32:43Z  
We compare three star formation rate (SFR) tracers --- far-ultraviolet (FUV), far-infrared (FIR) and 1.4\,GHz radio emission --- for \Nsamp\ galaxies (median redshift 0.12) from the Herschel Astrophysical Terahertz Large Area Survey Data Release 1 catalogue, excluding quasars, AGN-dominated galaxies and unreliable redshifts. Energy-balance spectral energy distribution fitting with \textsc{cigale} gives their attenuations, dust luminosities and SFRs self-consistently. The FUV attenuation correlates strongly with FIR luminosity (Spearman $ρ=0.84$), largely through their common dependence on redshift (partial $ρ=0.57$ at fixed $z$), and more tightly with the infrared excess ($ρ=0.96$). The uncorrected UV SFR is over ten times below the FIR SFR for 67 per cent of the galaxies, and for all with $A_{\rm FUV}&gt;4$\,mag. For 714 galaxies the FIRST 1.4\,GHz flux density is at the noise level and is treated as an upper limit; as detections, these values push the fits to unrealistically low dust temperatures. The 84 radio detections have a median $q_{\rm IR}$ of 2.51, close to the assumed 2.58. SFRs averaged over 10 and 100\,Myr differ by under 2 per cent, because the model star formation histories are smooth. Without access to high-performance computing, all galaxies were fitted at $z=0$, with distance-dependent quantities rescaled. This lowers dust temperatures by a factor of about $(1+z)$ and biases stellar masses and, at $z&gt;0.2$, attenuations, but changes dust luminosities and SFRs by only a few hundredths of a dex. These fits, with 16.3 million models per galaxy, show what UV-to-radio SED modelling can deliver on modest hardware.

We present a spatially resolved search for off-nuclear active galactic nucleus (AGN) candidates in the MaNGA survey. Using megacubes, we analyze over 10,000 galaxies, separating AGN activity from diffuse ionized gas with refined equivalent-width and surface-brightness thresholds. Combining a spatial radial algorithm with BPT, WHaN, and WHaD diagnostics, we classify 261 nuclear AGNs, 201 off-nuclear AGNs, and 159 'quiet' (composite-spaxel-dominated) sources. EuclidFM: Foundation Model for Compact Photometric Survey Representation  
Reyhaneh Vojoudi Salmani, Mahsa Berahman, Saeed Ansarifard, Michael M. Fausnaugh, Seyed Mohammad Sadegh Movahed  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25080v1  
Published: 2026-11-18T19:21:05Z  
We present EuclidFM, a compact self-supervised model that learns object-level representations from heterogeneous catalogue photometry in Euclid Quick Data Release 1. Using 90,258 sources across three Euclid Deep Fields, a masked-autoencoder Transformer reconstructs withheld fluxes and uncertainties from available measurements, producing a 128-dimensional embedding per source. The embeddings encode morphological and photometric information: sources with high point-like probability have, on average, 12.4 similarly classified neighbours among their 15 nearest neighbours, compared with 4.3 under field- and magnitude-stratified randomization. Linear models trained on frozen embeddings predict VIS magnitudes with correlations of 0.954-0.980 across three held-out fields. For sources with VIS aperture-flux uncertainties up to 1 microJy, incorporating embeddings reduces the root-mean-square error in predicted log uncertainty by at least 24% relative to a magnitude-only model. A classifier trained on spectroscopically confirmed sources identifies 1,150 quasar candidates, but only 21 of the 156 candidates with accepted external counterparts show AGN evidence, including 12 spectroscopic quasars. The classifier's transferability to the wider photometric catalogue therefore remains uncertain, and its purity and completeness require further evaluation. Independently of classification, EuclidFM provides a common representation for downstream survey analyses when photometric coverage differs between sources. Code and data are available at https://github.com/AI-Driven-HEP/EuclidFM.  

A PhEW Good Simulations: I. Galaxy Properties  
Lisiyuan Yang, Neal Katz, Evan Scannapieco, Aditi Vijayan  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21716v2  
Published: 2026-11-16T19:00:01Z  
Although galactic outflows driven by stellar feedback are widely implemented in modern cosmological simulations, the interactions between them and the circumgalactic medium (CGM) remains poorly resolved. To overcome this limitation, we conduct a suite of cosmological simulations that use the PhEW (Physically Evolved Winds) subgrid model, which analytically tracks the exchange of mass, metals, momentum, and energy by winds with the ambient CGM using an algorithm based on high-resolution cloud-crushing simulations. Our results show that even without AGN feedback, our simulations are able to accurately reproduce the observed galactic stellar mass functions (GSMFs) and stellar mass-halo mass (SMHM) relations at $z\gtrsim 1$, after which the massive galaxies in our simulations start to overproduce stars. Our simulations also match the observed relations of $M_{\mathrm{HI}}-M_*$, $M_{\mathrm{H_2}}-M_*$, $Z_*-M_*$ and $Z_{\mathrm{ISM}}-M_*$. Taken together these results suggest that AGN feedback is much less important for galaxy formation and evolution than previously believed. However, like found in many previous simulations, the specific star formation rates (sSFRs) of our galaxies are significantly lower than those observed at $z\gtrsim1$ at all stellar masses. Significantly, we find that our results are robust against changes in the resolution and volume of our simulations, without the need to retune our wind parameters.
