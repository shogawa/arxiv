A Population Synthesis Model for Odd Radio Circles Produced by Shock-Bubble Interactions  
Yiting Wang, Sebastian Heinz, Alexander Tchekhovskoy  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32916v1  
Published: 2026-10-23T17:18:02Z  
Odd Radio Circles (ORCs) are edge-brightened rings of diffuse radio emission of roughly arcminute size whose physical origin remains debated. One proposed mechanism is vortex formation through Shock-Bubble interaction (SBI), in which a merger or accretion shock re-energizes a fossil radio lobe into an edge-brightened ring. We test whether this mechanism can reproduce the observed ORC abundance using a Monte Carlo population-synthesis model that combines the galaxy stellar mass function, the radio-AGN incidence relation, dark-matter halo merger histories, and the inflation and buoyant survival of AGN-inflated bubbles, with synthetic radio fluxes and ring geometries drawn from a grid of magnetohydrodynamic SBI simulations. Applying survey-specific selection to the ASKAP-EMU Pilot Survey (944 MHz) and the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey (LoTSS; 144 MHz), we predict $2.7^{+3.9}_{-1.6}$ detectable ORCs in the EMU Pilot, matching the three found there, and $268^{+331}_{-148}$ in LoTSS, of which $7.5^{+9.5}_{-4.2}$ have a counterpart at 1.4 GHz. The predicted hosts are mostly central galaxies in group-scale halos. The predicted rings are physically smaller and lie at lower redshifts than the galaxies identified as hosts of the known ORCs, suggesting that SBI produces mainly the nearby part of the ORC population, or that some ORCs are associated with the wrong host. The population synthesis model also predicts a large population of low surface brightness steep-spectrum ORCs in the LoTSS band, implying a large hidden population and predicting that deeper low-frequency surveys should uncover many more ORCs than are currently known. These results indicate that shock-bubble interactions can account for at least part of the observed incidence of ORCs.  

Local Analogs of Little Red Dots: Clues to the X-ray Weakness  
Shi-Jiang Chen, Jun-Xian Wang, Zhen-Ya Zheng, Ruqiu Lin, Luis C. Ho, Zhen-Yi Cai, Chang-Hao Chen, Zsofi Igo, Jia-Lai Kang, Ruancun Li, Teng Liu, Wenke Ren, Zhen-Bo Su  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32430v1  
Published: 2026-10-23T09:55:34Z  
A major question about JWST's little red dots (LRDs) at $z\sim4-8$ is the origin of their apparent X-ray weakness. Even deep Chandra stacks yield only tentative detections or broad-band limits and cannot distinguish among super-Eddington accretion, intrinsic X-ray weakness, and Compton-thick (CT) obscuration. Local analogs offer a powerful complementary route. At $z\sim0.3$, similarly luminous sources require $\sim10^{3-4}$ less exposure for comparable signal-to-noise, suffer less source confusion, and directly probe the diagnostic rest-frame soft X-rays. Here we present a pilot Einstein Probe/FXT study of six "local little red dots" (LLRDs), V-shaped broad-line green pea galaxies selected as low-redshift counterparts of LRDs. These sources show X-ray diversity: "X-normal" LLRDs resemble type 1 AGNs in X-ray luminosity and optical variability, whereas the "X-weak" stack lies $\sim0.5-0.7$ dex below standard unobscured-quasar scaling relations while remaining compatible with the representative LRD stack and 95% of published individual limits. The X-weak LLRDs also lack detected optical variability and have redder optical continua than X-normal LLRDs, making their V-shaped SEDs closer to those of high-redshift LRDs. The detection of the X-weak stack at $\sim3.3σ$ with the soft-X-ray-sensitive EP-FXT provides rest-frame $\sim2-3$ keV constraints difficult to reconcile with a typical unobscured super-Eddington model or a local-AGN-like CT geometry examined here, although modified variants remain possible. The simplest alternative is a mildly X-ray-weak corona ($Δα_{\mathrm{ox}}\sim-0.1$ to $-0.2$) with Compton-thin absorption ($N_{\mathrm{H}}\sim10^{22-23}$ cm$^{-2}$), consistent with the independent Balmer decrement/break diagnostics. Together with growing samples of local LRD analogs, our results offer a practical path for testing the physical nature of LRD X-ray weakness.  

Non-Axisymmetric Pillar Structures due to Embedded Stars in AGN Accretion Discs  
Jiamu Huang, Douglas N. C. Lin, Keith Horne  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31751v1  
Published: 2026-10-22T18:00:01Z  
Gravitationally unstable outer regions of AGN discs may host in situ formation of massive stars whose heating produces localized vertical "pillars" on the disc surface, in the same region that emits the broad lines. Here we investigate the reverberation-mapping signatures that such pillars would produce. We compute velocity-delay maps $Ψ(λ, τ)$, time-evolving emission-line spectra, and continuum reverberation lag spectra for these structures. We model each pillar as a 2D Gaussian perturbation to the disc height under lamp-post irradiation and weight the line response with Cloudy photoionization grids covering the major BLR lines. We show H$α$, Mg II, and C IV as representative examples. A single pillar produces a localized hotspot in $Ψ$ and a clear "S-wave" pattern in the time-evolving spectrum that persists, though diluted, for $N \sim 100$ pillars. Emission-line fluxes are suppressed in shadows behind the pillars, but when normalized by H$β$ (or by the local continuum) the line-to-H$β$ ratios move in opposite directions: Mg II/H$β$ rises while C IV/H$β$ drops. This contrast distinguishes pillars from purely kinematic features that would modulate all lines by similar factors. Across scans of the pillar shape and lamp-post height, the pillar models produce an optical continuum reverberation lag spectrum of the observed magnitude. The once-per-orbit modulation grows with $\sin i$ from foreshortening, and at sufficiently high inclination the pillar's body geometrically self-occults its lamp-facing inner slope, adding a sharper near/far contrast. These signatures provide observational diagnostics for detecting in situ star formation in AGN discs through velocity-resolved reverberation mapping campaigns.  

An Extra-nuclear Water Maser in NGC 1106 Likely Driven By An AGN Shock: Implications for Maser-based Black Hole Mass Measurements  
Macon Magno, Emmanuel Momjian, Krista L. Smith, O. Ivy Wong, Travis C. Fischer, Michael J. Koss, Chenoa D. Tremblay  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31753v1  
Published: 2026-10-22T18:00:01Z  
We present Very Large Array A-configuration ($0.13$'') 22 GHz spectral-line and continuum observations of the Swift-BAT detected Seyfert 2 galaxy, NGC 1106, aimed at localizing a water maser line offset from the host systemic velocity by 541 km s$^{-1}$. The 22 GHz continuum emission reveals a 352 pc extended structure within the nucleus of NGC 1106, and we spatially resolve the water maser emission to a compact region $\sim1$ kpc ($3.12$'') from the nucleus. Comparing our data with previous 22 GHz 1'' observations, we found that the water maser lies near the boundary of a radio extension aligned with the jet axis, likely arising from the interface between the active galactic nucleus (AGN) outflow and the host galaxy's ISM. To verify this, we compared the water maser in NGC 1106 to a known water maser in a star-forming region within the Milky Way, along with known extragalactic water masers associated with star formation and AGN driven outflows. Overall, we found that the maser position, velocity offset, flux density, and alignment with the jet axis are consistent with an origin at the interface between the AGN outflow and the host ISM. Our results have important implications for maser-based black hole mass measurements in which disk geometry is ambiguous. Since single-dish observations cannot spatially separate disk masers from outflow-driven masers, high-velocity maser features attributed to the accretion disk may also include contributions from kpc-scale shocks.
