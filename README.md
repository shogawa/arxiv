URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17359v1  
Published: 2026-10-12T20:15:56Z  
How galaxies assemble depends on processes ranging from small-scale baryonic physics to large-scale cosmological assembly, which are difficult to disentangle with observations alone. Using $\sim$63,000 simulated galaxies from seven cosmological simulations spanning hydrodynamical, semi-analytic, and semi-empirical models, we explore how specific star formation histories (sSFHs) vary across modelling assumptions. Using non-linear dimensionality reduction (UMAP), we find that sSFH shape variation organizes along a few smooth axes in a 2D embedding, broadly corresponding to (1) peak timing, (2) peak sharpness, and (3) subsequent quenching rate. These results provide avenues for theoretical and observational follow-up connecting star formation histories to subgrid physical models.

This study extends the optical/X-ray correlation from black hole low-mass X-ray binaries (XRBs) to active galactic nuclei (AGN), bridging scales from stellar-mass to supermassive black holes (SMBHs). In XRBs, optical emission predominantly originates from the thermal reprocessing of high-energy photons on the surface of the geometrically thin, optically thick accretion disk. This scaling relation provides new constraints for models of X-ray emission regions in accreting black holes.

Anisotropic Radiation in Super-Eddington Active Galactic Nuclei: The Responsivity of Broad-Line Regions to Continuum Variability

URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15255v1  
Published: 2026-10-11T00:53:44Z  
Anisotropic ionizing radiation is a natural theoretical expectation for super-Eddington accretion in active galactic nuclei (AGNs), implying that the ionizing flux incident on the broad-line region (BLR) clouds differs from that measured along the observer's line of sight. Reverberation mapping studies have revealed systematically shortened time lags of the broad H$β$ emission line relative to continuum variations in super-Eddington AGNs, a phenomenon that can be naturally interpreted as a consequence of anisotropic ionizing continuum radiation. To further investigate this anisotropy, we perform a systematic analysis of the H$β$ line responsivity, defined as the ratio of the variability amplitude of H$β$ to that of the driving continuum, for both sub- and super-Eddington AGNs. By combining the effective responsivities measured relative to the driving continuum, after correcting for the continuum--emission-line delay, with photoionization calculations, we infer the ionizing radiation field received by their BLRs. We find that, in super-Eddington AGNs, the ionizing flux inferred from the H$β$ responsivity differs significantly from that observed along the line of sight, whereas the two are in closer agreement in sub-Eddington AGNs. These results provide compelling evidence for strong anisotropy of the incident ionizing radiation in super-Eddington AGNs. We conclude by discussing the broader implications of this anisotropy, including its potential relevance to the "little red dots" recently discovered by the James Webb Space Telescope.
