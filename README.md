Tracing the Epoch of Reionization through extreme emission line galaxies around Cosmic Noon  
M. Delgado Mancheño, J. Gallego, S. Pascual, M. Tang, R. Pello, N. Laporte, A. Camps-Fariña, M. Chamorro-Cazorla  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14332v1  
Published: 2026-11-10T16:56:06Z  
Star-forming galaxies at z&gt;6 are thought to be the main drivers of cosmic reionization, but their faintness makes detailed study challenging even with JWST. Extreme Emission Line Galaxies (EELGs) are compact, low-mass systems with intense nebular emission and are potential lower-redshift analogues of this population. We aim to assemble and characterize a statistically significant sample of EELGs in the CANDELS/EGS field around cosmic noon, and to assess their validity as analogues of the galaxies that drove reionization. We select EELG candidates from CANDELS photometry with a J-band color-excess criterion that identifies both H$β$+[OIII] and H$α$ emitters. Physical properties are taken from CANDELS data products, and the selection is validated with MMT/MMIRS, Keck/MOSFIRE, HST/WFC3 and JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy, complemented by our own GTC/OSIRIS+ program. We identify 102 H$β$+[OIII] emitters at $z\sim1.7$ and 127 H$α$ emitters at $z\sim1.0$. They are compact ($\sim$1.6 kpc), low-mass ($\log(M_\star/M_\odot)\sim8$) and strongly star-forming (sSFR $\sim10^{-8}$ yr$^{-1}$). Estimated purities of $84.3\pm1.5$\% (H$β$+[OIII]) and $76.8\pm2.0$\% (H$α$) agree with spectroscopic confirmation rates (85\% and 63\%), with no evidence of significant AGN contamination. A joint Bayesian fit, corrected for completeness via a truncated likelihood, yields $\log(M_\star/M_\odot) = (6.71\pm0.16) + (3.31\pm0.46)\log(1+z)$, suggesting an evolution in the characteristic mass of the sample with cosmic time. We spectroscopically confirm a highly pure, AGN-free sample of cosmic-noon EELGs. While their mass scale evolves with redshift, their properties consistently match those of z&gt;5 JWST extreme emitters, supporting them as intermediate-redshift analogues.

We present results from deep XMM-Newton observations ($\sim$180 ksec) of the galaxy group IC 2476 in the X-GAP sample, capturing a rare AGN feedback regime, where the central radio AGN has ceased activity but the radio lobes are still detectable. The hot intragroup medium of IC 2476 features a compact cool core, with a size of ~10 kpc, and a moderate X-ray luminosity of $\sim3.4\times10^{40}$ erg/s. Jingbo Sun, Hengxiao Guo, Sjoert van Velzen, Yuhan Yao, Mar Mezcua, Stefan Geier, Alok C. Gupta, Karan Dogra, Philip G. Edwards, Wenwen Zuo, Yu Pan, Minfeng Gu  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13364v1  
Published: 2026-11-09T23:38:02Z  
TDE 2025abcr is a tidal disruption event (TDE) located approximately 9 kpc in projection from its host galaxy nucleus. Our spectroscopic follow-up with the Very Large Telescope (VLT) reveals C IV $λ\lambda5801, 5812$ emission, representing the first identification of this C IV doublet in a TDE. These transitions are uncommon in active galactic nuclei (AGN) spectra but have been observed in supernova flash spectra and Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars. We propose that the doublet arises from the combined effects of continuum fluorescence and photon trapping in the 312 Å transition, analogous to the mechanism operating in nitrogen-sequence Wolf-Rayet (WN) winds. This mechanism requires intense extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) illumination and a high optical depth, providing a distinctive diagnostic of the physical conditions in optically thick TDE outflows. These requirements, together with the relatively narrow widths of the broad emission lines, the presence of Bowen fluorescence features, and the small inferred blackbody radius, favor a compact and dense envelope in TDE 2025abcr. The optical C IV doublet may have been overlooked in previous observational and theoretical studies of TDEs, motivating more detailed radiative-transfer calculations for optically thick outflows. With future TDE samples from Rubin/LSST and WFST, a larger C IV TDE sample will test if this population systematically favors a compact photosphere.

Lena Saurenhaus, Francesca Capel, Foteini Oikonomou  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13247v1  
Published: 2026-11-09T21:21:21Z  
Recent evidence for high-energy neutrino emission from NGC 1068 and other nearby Seyfert galaxies suggests that active galactic nuclei (AGNs) may constitute a class of astrophysical neutrino sources. Using a physically motivated neutrino emission model based on stochastic particle acceleration in magnetized AGN coronae, we investigate the detection prospects of X-ray-bright Seyfert galaxies with a detector network comprising IceCube, Baikal-GVD, KM3NeT/ARCA, and P-ONE. We find that the Circinus Galaxy is the most promising point-source target in the Southern sky and will be detected at the 5$σ$ level by 2038 at the latest, provided that the Seyfert population accounts for the diffuse astrophysical neutrino flux up to 100 TeV and Circinus is at least as efficient a neutrino emitter as the average source in the population. Giant Radio Galaxies from the Third Data Release of the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey (LoTSS-DR3): Physical Characteristics and Environments

URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13185v1  
Published: 2026-11-09T20:05:58Z  
Giant radio galaxies (GRGs) are radio-loud active galactic nuclei (AGN) with projected linear sizes exceeding 0.7 Mpc, offering insights into the evolution of large-scale radio jets and their environments. We present 2333 GRGs from the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey Data Release 3 (LoTSS-DR3), with projected sizes of 0.7-2.9 Mpc, redshifts of $0.126 \leq z \leq 3.517$, and 150-MHz radio luminosities of $1.72 \times 10^{24}$-$3.04 \times 10^{28}$ W Hz$^{-1}$. Depletion of Dark Matter Spikes by Massive Black Hole Mergers  
Charlie Sharpe, Thomas F. M. Spieksma, Yonadav Barry Ginat, Ricarda S. Beckmann, Alexander Heger, Bence Kocsis  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13170v1  
Published: 2026-11-09T19:53:37Z  
Dark matter over-densities around massive black holes, known as dark matter spikes, can dephase gravitational waves emitted by intermediate- and extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (IMRIs/EMRIs), offering a promising way to indirectly detect dark matter. This detection channel, however, requires the spike to survive over cosmological time-scales. We investigate the impact that a host black hole's merger history has on its dark-matter spike. We focus on how the inspiral of a massive-black-hole (MBH) binary modifies and disrupts these spikes within the central $\sim 100$ Schwarzschild radii of the black hole. We find that a single merger with mass ratio $q = 0.03$ is capable of reducing a spike's density by around 3 (5) orders of magnitude at 100 (5) Schwarzschild radii across all polar angles relative to the binary's plane, independent of the primary MBH's mass, with larger mass ratios inducing even stronger depletion. In fact, a single past merger with mass ratio as small as $q = 0.005$ reduces central densities by 2--3 orders of magnitude, making gravitational-wave dephasing difficult to detect. We relate these results to the MBH merger history by analysing cosmological simulation data from Horizon-AGN. We find that mergers become increasingly common as the MBH mass increases, with mergers of mass ratio q &gt; 0.03 having occurred in 50% of MBHs of mass $3\times10^7M_\odot$ and 95% of mass $10^8M_\odot$ at z=0. These fractions are reached at even lighter masses ($2\times10^7M_\odot$ and $5\times10^7M_\odot$, respectively) at z=3. We find that accretion-induced adiabatic MBH growth is unable to cause appreciable spike re-growth between such mergers in {\tt Horizon-AGN}. Thus, in combination with previous studies of EMRI-induced depletion, this work constrains the mass range over which massive black holes can host detectable DM spikes to $10^6M_\odot$--$5 \times 10^7M_\odot$ for $z \leq 3$.

The Edge of the Warm Corona in Active Galactic Nuclei

A model of warm, dissipative corona on top of an accretion disk is investigated, to explain the soft X-ray excess (SXE) emission observed in active galactic nuclei (AGN). The final ray-traced spectrum, computed through photoionization code with Comptonization, accounts for both: emitted and reflected components. As a result, the strength of the SXE depends on the radial extend of the warm corona, i.e., its outer edge. We show that the radial transition from warm corona to disk atmosphere is governed by line cooling. By moving away from a central black hole Compton cooling is gradually replaced by line cooling, and the temperature drops sharply in vertical direction at a narrow iron ionization front, where iron ions Fe \textsc{xxv}--Fe \textsc{xxvi} recombine to Fe \textsc{xvii}. Notably, this transition takes place at a particular value of internal heating $\approx 1.6 \times 10^{-23}$ erg cm$^{3}$ s$^{-1}$, regardless of the Eddington ratio. We show, that at the given metal abundance, the SXE emitting area is larger for a higher accretion rate, in agreement with recent findings in changing look AGN. The overall emission spectrum from a warm corona is featureless, and the only iron K$_α$ line comes from a reflection. The line is stronger and more gravitationally distorted for a lower accretion rate, which is a direct result of the size of the warm corona. Furthermore, by increasing the iron abundance, the iron ionization front moves closer towards black hole, and a stronger iron line complex is emitted locally.
