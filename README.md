Effect of galaxy mergers on dust masses  
W. J. Pearson, M. Romano, K. Małek, D. Donevski, K. Lisiecki, A. P. Cotter, S. Dey, T. Goto, S. C. -C. Ho, D. J. Santos  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26166v1  
Published: 2026-10-19T10:56:57Z  
Dust mass (M$_{Dust}$) is one of the key physical properties of galaxies that track the cold interstellar medium. However, the M$_{Dust}$ in merging galaxies is poorly understood. We aim to derive how the cold dust mass of galaxies is affected by major mergers at low redshifts. M$_{Dust}$ was estimated for approximately 500 major mergers in the North Ecliptic Pole extragalactic field using spectral energy distribution fitting runs with CIGALE, with different dust attenuation and emission models. We also analysed over 12 000 mergers from the SIMBA simulation using its M$_{Dust}$. We matched merging and non-merging systems in stellar mass, redshift, and local density, to enable a comparison of M$_{Dust}$. We then performed a second comparison using a control sample also matched in SFR. AGN host galaxies were then removed, and this matching repeated. Observed mergers have larger M$_{Dust}$ than the non-mergers by up to a factor of 1.82$\pm$0.35, or 1.60$\pm$0.44 without AGN-hosts. When matched by SFR, observed mergers show no significant change in M$_{Dust}$, both when including and excluding AGN-hosts. SIMBA galaxies show similar trends when AGN-hosts are included but there is still an increase in M$_{Dust}$ when matched by SFR. When AGN-hosts are excluded, the SIMBA mergers show smaller M$_{Dust}$ by a factor of 0.98$\pm$0.03, or 0.97$\pm$0.02 when matched in SFR. The higher the mass of a merger, the greater M$_{Dust}$ increase for observations for two of the CIGALE runs, or the smaller the decrease for SIMBA. Observed mergers are typically more dusty than their non-merging counterparts but only because they typically have higher SFRs, regardless of whether they host AGN or not. SIMBA mergers follow observations when AGN hosts are included, but show reduced M$_{Dust}$ when AGN hosts are removed. M$_{Dust}$ in SIMBA is linked to AGN in a way not seen in observations.  

Velocity-resolved Ionization Mapping of the Broad-line Region. II. Signatures of Eccentric Broad-line Regions from a Quantitative Photoionization-Based Model  
Jiancheng Wu, Hai-Cheng Feng, Sha-Sha Li, Qingwen Wu, Xinwu Cao  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25718v1  
Published: 2026-10-19T03:11:16Z  
Velocity-resolved reverberation mapping (RM) is one of the most powerful tools for probing the geometry and kinematics of the broad-line region in active galactic nuclei. However, its interpretation may be non-unique, as similar velocity-delay signatures can arise from different combinations of BLR geometry and kinematics. To address this degeneracy, this series of studies develops velocity-resolved ionization mapping (IM) as a complementary technique. In this second paper of the series, we construct photoionization-based BLR models to quantitatively demonstrate the diagnostic power of the joint RM+IM framework. We calculate local \ha\ and \hb\ emissivities with Cloudy, couple them with disk-like BLR models, and generate synthetic velocity-dependent line fluxes, lags, and Balmer decrements. The model reproduces the observed anti-correlation between the integrated BD and the incident ionizing continuum, with scatter naturally introduced by reverberation delays. We show that a virialized elliptical BLR can produce diverse velocity-delay maps and velocity-resolved lags that mimic RM signatures commonly attributed to virialized motion, inflow, or outflow. In contrast, the velocity-resolved BD is sensitive to the symmetry of the BLR gas distribution, providing a key diagnostic for distinguishing eccentric geometry from non-virial motions. Through proof-of-concept comparisons with one double-peaked and one single-peaked AGN, we show that an elliptical-disk BLR can simultaneously reproduce the observed line profiles, velocity-resolved lags, and velocity-resolved BDs. These results provide a quantitative basis for IM, support the presence of eccentric BLR structures in AGNs, and demonstrate the potential of joint RM+IM analyses to break the BLR geometry-kinematics degeneracy.  

Tidal Disruption Events in Active Galactic Nuclei: Thermal Radiation and Jets  
Nimrod Tripto, Tsvi Piran, Julian Krolik  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24849v1  
Published: 2026-10-17T19:54:37Z  
Over the past decade, dozens of Tidal Disruption Events (TDEs) have been identified, with many more expected from upcoming surveys. A small subset has been observed to launch transient, highly luminous relativistic jets, for which the origin of the requisite magnetic flux has not been identified. A non-negligible fraction of TDEs are expected in Active Galactic Nuclei (AGNs), where a magnetized accretion flow is already present. As shown first by Chan et al. (2019), the collision of a TDE debris stream with this flow can substantially alter both disk and debris dynamics, leading to a possibly Eddington-limited flare. Here, we use the Athena++ radiation magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) code to simulate the time near peak fallback rate of a TDE in an AGN, studying the debris-disk interaction, the radiation emitted, and the accumulation of magnetic flux near the horizon. In the example simulated, the accretion rate climbs to $\sim 12\,\dot{M}_{ \rm Edd}$, while the escaping solid angle-integrated luminosity approaches $\sim L_{ \rm Edd}$. The photon luminosity is strongly asymmetric, brighter in the hemisphere from which the debris strikes the disk and dimmer in the hemisphere toward which the debris travels. The photon spectrum peaks in the EUV. The polar magnetic flux grows $\sim 33$-fold, enough to raise the Blandford--Znajek luminosity by a factor $\sim 10^3$, reaching a level sufficient to power observed TDE jets. The polar magnetic flux is also asymmetric, likely making any jet similarly asymmetric, but in the opposite sense to the photon flux.  

The Impact of an AGN on PAH Spectral Features in Galaxy Evolution Simulations: Continuum Dilution and Excitation of PAHs  
Grant P. Donnelly, Desika Narayanan, Paul Torrey, J. -D. T. Smith, Helena M. Richie, Hui Li, Federico Marinacci  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24778v1  
Published: 2026-10-17T16:42:32Z  
Galaxies hosting an active galactic nucleus (AGN) show diminished power and non-standard spectral feature ratios from polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) compared with normal star-forming galaxies. Proposed mechanisms for this difference include a combination of AGN-driven PAH grain processing, differential heating from the surrounding radiation field, and continuum dilution. These potentially competing factors are difficult to disentangle observationally. In this paper, we investigate the impact of the AGN radiation field on the excitation properties and potential dilution of the mid-IR emission features via constrained numerical experiments of idealized galaxies in simulations that include a self-consistent model for the evolution of dust. We find that, for the integrated spectrum of our simulated Milky Way analog and independent of grain-processing physics, the apparent PAH emission is progressively diminished as AGN luminosity is increased. For spectra within the central half kiloparsec, PAH features are entirely obfuscated when an AGN of any sampled luminosity is present. We additionally find that the UV radiation from an AGN is capable of impacting PAH band ratios, though note that we do not yet include a model for potential photodestruction of PAHs. Our model suggests that the inferred lack of PAHs via featureless mid-IR spectra from galaxies hosting AGN may simply owe to the direct continuum from the AGN overpowering mid-IR features from PAHs in the ISM of the host galaxy. Future work will investigate the relative role of AGN continuum dilution and PAH heating vs photodestruction/ionization of PAHs.  

Clumpy Outflows from Super-Eddington Accreting Black Holes. II. Conditions for Clump Formation  
Haojie Hu, Yuta Asahina, Shogo Yoshioka, Hiroyuki R. Takahashi, Ken Ohsuga  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24660v1  
Published: 2026-10-17T12:22:08Z  
Active galactic nuclei (AGNs) commonly exhibit ultra-fast outflows (UFOs) with velocities of $0.03$--$0.3~c$. Recent high-resolution X-ray observations with XRISM/Resolve suggest that UFOs may have a clumpy structure. However, the formation and evolution of these clumpy outflows remain poorly understood. In the first paper of this series, we investigated the formation of clumpy outflows using numerical simulations and find that sufficiently high spatial resolution is required to reproduce clumpy structures numerically. In this second paper, we perform a series of numerical experiments to identify the key physical processes governing clump formation. We find that spatial fluctuations of physical quantities at the disk surface are essential for the formation of clumpy outflows, whereas spatial and temporal fluctuations at the inner boundary have only a minor effect. Furthermore, density structure-function analysis suggests that the high-density regions have an elongated morphology with characteristic transverse widths of several tens of $R_{\rm g}$, possibly produced by radiation Rayleigh--Taylor instability. A more comprehensive investigation of the instability in general-relativistic radiation magnetohydrodynamic simulations, where accretion disk structures emerge self-consistently, is expected to provide deeper insights into the physical origin of clumpy outflows.  

Modeling Multi-band Active Galactic Nuclei Lightcurves Using an Self-supervised Attentive Neural Process Network  
Tzuken Shen, Chengcheng Xin, Joshua Fagin, Ashley Villar, Matthew J. Graham, K. E. Saavik Ford  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24195v1  
Published: 2026-10-16T20:12:41Z  
With the the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), observations of tens of millions of quasars are expected. We present a machine-learning framework based on an attentive neural process (ANP) that models stochastic quasar variability and jointly performs two tasks: (i) probabilistic reconstruction of quasar light curves in all six LSST bands, and (ii) inference of physical parameters of the AGN system --- black hole mass, Eddington ratio, and intrinsic variability timescale --- with joint, full-covariance posteriors. The model processes all six bands within a single masked attention mechanism, so that observations in any band constrain the shared driving process, and natively handles irregularly and asynchronously sampled light curves with realistic heteroscedastic noise. We train and evaluate on full ten-year light curves simulated with a relativistic accretion-disk reprocessing model, observed through the fielded Rubin baseline cadence with per-visit photometric noise. The light-curve model is trained with self-supervised, learning from the sparse noisy observations alone and never accessing the underlying noiseless light curves. On a held-out test set, we show that our ANP model reach comparable performances to that of the near-optimal baseline Gaussian process model, while exceeding it in power spectrum reconstruction and damped-random walk timescale estimation. The compact learned latent representation supports future downstream applications, and the fully observation-driven training objective enables direct fine-tuning on real LSST light curves.
