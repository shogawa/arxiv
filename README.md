Aims. We investigated the potential large-scale alignment of radio active galactic nuclei (AGN) jets in the proximity of the Shapley Supercluster (SSC) area and its correlation with the identified cosmic web structures. A statistical study of ionized outflows in low-mass galaxies from DESI DR1  
Michael Romano, Amélie Saintonge, Chiara Circosta, Claudia Cicone, Darko Donevski, Dirk Scholte, Bendix Hagedorn  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15884v1

We present a statistical analysis of ionized gas outflows in a large sample of low-mass ($\mathrm{log(M_{*}/M_{\odot})\lesssim10}$) galaxies at $z&lt;0.45$ from the first data release of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI DR1). Using [OIII] and H$α$ emission lines, we identify $\sim5800$ dwarf galaxies with robust signatures of ionized outflows. Most are star-forming systems above the star-forming main sequence, with a smaller fraction of composites and AGN hosts. The derived mass-loading factors span $η=0.001-1$, with a median $η\sim0.01$, consistent with previous studies at similar stellar masses. These values are $1$--$2$ dex lower than predicted by cosmological simulations, suggesting that the ionized phase traces only a minor fraction of the total outflow mass. Outflow velocities are sufficient to enrich the circumgalactic medium in $\gtrsim80\%$ of cases, while escape into the intergalactic medium appears efficient mainly in composite and AGN hosts. We find that mass-loading factors decrease with stellar mass, broadly reproducing simulated trends despite the normalization offset. Comparing individual detections with stacked spectra of the general low-mass star-forming population, we find systematically lower mass-loading factors in the stacks, although the difference decreases toward larger offsets above the star-forming main sequence. Together with a positive correlation between mass-loading factor and specific star-formation rate, this suggests that enhanced star formation and burstiness regulate feedback efficiency and shape galactic winds. Our results highlight the role of ionized outflows in the baryon cycle of low-mass galaxies and the need for multiphase studies to fully understand feedback in galaxy evolution.

For the Rubin WFD survey, we estimate that periodic oscillations in microlensing light curves could identify $\sim0.12-0.68$ short-period ($&lt;5$\,yr) binaries, whereas diagnostics based on the reconstructed size-wavelength relation apply to a broader population, potentially identifying $\sim0.4-5.3$ systems, depending on the model assumptions. CSST, Euclid, and Roman may also reveal up to $10.6$, $8.9$, and $1.2$ systems exhibiting size-wavelength features, and $2.3$, $1.9$, $0.3$ systems showing periodic oscillations, which should be regarded as imaging-selected candidates whose microlensing-based identification requires adequate time-domain follow-up, including cadence, monitoring baseline, photometric precision, and reliable time-delay measurements. Our results show that strongly lensed AGNs provide a promising, though observationally demanding, pathway for finding candidate SMBBHs via microlensing.
