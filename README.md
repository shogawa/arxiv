Constraining Supermassive Black Hole Seeding and Accretion via Occupation Fractions and Mass-Weighted Distributions  
Gianni Sims, Vicki Sarajedini  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35673v1  
Published: 2026-10-26T16:14:58Z  
The formation of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) is a key open problem in galaxy evolution, with light- and heavy-seeding models predicting distinct black hole occupation fractions (BHOFs). Using multiwavelength observations from the LSST Deep Drilling Fields, we measure the AGN fraction and estimate the BHOF across $0&lt;z&lt;1$. Applying a hierarchical Bayesian model, we find that the inferred BHOF remains $100\%$ for host galaxy masses below $10^9 M_\odot$ at lower redshifts. These findings are consistent with light-seed scenarios. Additionally, we computed the black hole masses for several galaxies in the W-CDF-S to test the mass-weighted black hole occupation distributions from semi-analytic models (SAM). Using a reduced-$χ^2$ analysis, we find that the data are consistent with the main-sequence accretion model regardless of the assumed seeding mechanism. These results provide new observational constraints on SMBH seeding and highlight the value of combining deep extragalactic surveys with probabilistic modeling.

The Peanut galaxy cluster (SRGe CL0238.3+2005) offers a rare opportunity to investigate thermal and nonthermal processes only $\sim0.1$\,Gyr after pericentric passage. We present a multi-frequency radio study of this system at $z=0.42$ using new MeerKAT and uGMRT observations, complemented by Chandra and MUSTANG-2 data. We identify two morphologically and spectrally distinct radio components: a double-lobed source in the northern subcluster and extended diffuse emission across the cluster center. The double-lobed source has a strongly curved, ultra-steep spectrum and is offset by $\sim22$\,kpc from the nearest brightest cluster galaxy. Its spectral curvature and positional offset are consistent with remnant AGN plasma that may have been displaced during the merger or reshaped by a shock. The diffuse emission extends over $\sim1$~Mpc and is classified as an ultra-steep spectrum ($α=-1.77\pm0.11$ between 400\,MHz and 1.28\,GHz) radio halo. Its volume-averaged radio emissivity, $(1.1\pm0.1)\times10^{-41}$ erg s$^{-1}$ cm$^{-3}$ Hz$^{-1}$ at 1.4\,GHz, is an order of magnitude higher than the characteristic value for other halos. The radial profile of the halo is well described by a single exponential fit and its radio emission correlates tightly with the X-ray, with a best-fit slope of $1.11\pm0.05$. Chandra and MUSTANG-2 observations reveal candidate surface brightness discontinuities, with the most prominent inner feature coinciding with a region of flatter spectral index, potentially tracing localized particle (re)-acceleration. The coexistence of an extended ultra-steep spectrum radio halo, high radio emissivity, remnant AGN plasma, and candidate X-ray/SZ discontinuities makes the Peanut cluster an important test case for understanding the evolution of diffuse radio emission and its connection to the dynamical state of the intracluster medium in early-stage mergers.

Fast Outflow Driven from the Magnetically Arrested Disc  
Taili Yi, Jia-Wen Li  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33443v1  
Published: 2026-10-24T04:36:04Z  
Fast outflows are commonly observed in accreting black hole systems, yet their launching mechanism remains debated, especially when the gas is highly ionized and radiation line driving becomes inefficient. We investigate magnetically driven outflows from magnetically arrested discs (MADs) using a steady, axisymmetric magnetocentrifugal outflow model. The outflow equations follow the classical trans-magnetosonic MHD framework, while the disc structural scalings and outflow-base conditions are motivated by general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics (GRMHD) simulations. We solve for the trans-magnetosonic outflow solutions along prescribed large-scale poloidal magnetic field lines. The outflows launched from the inner disc are strongly accelerated and the formal Newtonian solutions can enter the mildly relativistic regime. The outflow velocity decreases systematically with increasing footpoint radius, producing a radially stratified outflow in which inner streamlines reach UFO-like velocities while outer streamlines form slower components. We also find that the MAD solutions have a large magnetic lever arm $\tilde{l}=(R_{\rm A}/R)^2$ is typically several tens and can reach $\sim10^2$, which implies that the outflow can carry a large amount of the specific angular momentum of the disc material at its footpoint, and we find that about $55\%$ of the angular-momentum estimated between the outer and inner disc boundaries is removed by the magnetic outflow. The total mass-loss rate in the outflow is about $14\%$ of the disc mass-accretion rate in our fiducial model. These results suggest that the MADs provide a natural environment for producing both fast, radially stratified disc outflow and efficient disc angular-momentum removal. MAD-driven outflow may therefore provide a magnetic origin for ultra-fast absorbers in low-luminosity active galactic nuclei (AGNs) and X-ray binaries (XRBs).

The diversity of Type I AGNs can be organized along the Quasar Main Sequence (MS), a powerful framework primarily driven by the accretion rate and defined in the optical plane by the FWHM of $\mathrm{Hβ}$ and the Fe II strength ($R_{\rm FeII}$). Previous studies suggested that the UV Fe III evolves along this MS. However, a systematic analysis is still missing. We aim to characterize the UV Fe III emission across the MS, assess its potential as a tracer, and investigate how its properties depend on the conditions of the emitting gas. We analyzed the UV iron emission in 69 low-$z$ $(z \lesssim 1)$ AGNs observed with the Hubble Space Telescope. We fitted the 1700-2200 Angstrom range and investigated the main correlations using principal component analysis. We complemented with $\tt{CLOUDY}$ photoionization simulations to explore the dependence of the iron emission on gas density, ionization parameter, metallicity, microturbulence, column density, and spectral energy distribution. We find a positive correlation between the strengths of the UV iron emission and the optical $\mathrm{R_{FeII}}$ parameter. The variations in the Fe III multiplets demonstrate that a single template may not represent all quasars. Thus, characterizing the Fe III emission and its dependence on physical conditions is essential for improving the modeling of UV AGN spectra.  

Initial Conditions of Magnetized AGN Accretion Disks from Cloud Capture and Disruption  
Philip F. Hopkins  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33297v1  
Published: 2026-10-23T21:09:18Z  
Accretion onto supermassive black holes (BHs) from the ISM can occur via capture of cold gas 'clouds' (GMCs, clumps, or overdense structures). Unlike material in stellar tidal disruptions or X-ray binaries, or classical Bondi accretion, ISM clouds are strongly magnetized (plasma $β\equiv P_{\rm thermal}/P_{\rm magnetic} \ll 1$), with supersonic turbulence, and cool rapidly. We derive analytic scalings for the initial conditions of accretion disks formed by capture/disruption of such clouds, in three regimes: (1) low-mass BHs ($M_{\rm BH} \ll M_{\rm cl}$), where turbulent Bondi-Hoyle or hierarchical 'leapfrog' growth via sub-cloud encounters dominates; (2) comparable-mass encounters ($M_{\rm BH} \sim M_{\rm cl}$); and (3) massive BHs tidally disrupting small clouds ($M_{\rm BH} \gg M_{\rm cl}$). In each, the resulting disk is predicted to be initially toroidally-magnetically-dominated ($|B_φ| \gg |B_{z}|$, $β\ll 1$), geometrically thick ($H/R \sim 0.1-1$), and gravitationally stable ($Q \gtrsim 1$), with super-sonic, trans-Alfvenic turbulence. These conditions differ qualitatively from those assumed in classical accretion/$α$ disk theory, 'chaotic cold accretion', stellar tidal disruption, or Bondi-Hoyle accretion. At least initially, the toroidal field should be sustained by flux-freezing on timescales shorter than Parker or turbulent resistive losses. We examine how cloud mass spectra drive accretion rate distributions, duty cycles, and fueling rates, with integrated BH growth dominated by clouds of mass comparable to the BH. Given known cosmological evolution, we suggest a scenario linking (a) inefficient growth and wandering BHs in dwarf galaxies, (b) hyper-Eddington growth in little red dots, (c) peak quasar activity in massive gas-rich systems at cosmic noon, and (d) lower-luminosity AGN in gas-poor galaxies at late times.

Some high-redshift overdensities show an enhanced incidence of AGNs, suggesting that large-scale environment may influence supermassive black hole fuelling and triggering. Whether environment also affects AGN variability remains unclear. Using the Cosmic Himalayas (CH), an extreme concentration of 11 type 1 quasars at z=2.16-2.20, we investigate possible offsets in rest-frame ultraviolet variability relative to quasars with comparable redshifts, black hole masses, and Eddington ratios.
