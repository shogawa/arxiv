A precessing AGN with S-shaped morphology discovered in MALS  
J. D. Wagenveld, N. Gupta  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23738v1  
Published: 2026-10-16T16:47:47Z  
We present J1221$-$1354, a precessing AGN discovered in the MeerKAT Absorption Line Survey (MALS) using deep L-band (0.9--1.6\,GHz) radio data. Its `S'-shaped morphology, revealed by the excellent surface brightness sensitivity of MeerKAT, is consistent with jet precession. Ancillary optical and infrared data establish the source as a Type 2 AGN in a moderately overdense environment. The approaching (southern) jet is brighter but shorter than the receding jet, with the northern edge of the receding jet showing a significantly younger spectral age. These features are peculiar, but consistent with a jet reorientation event in an asymmetric ambient medium, in which the jet re-enters a cavity of previously deposited plasma. A simple ballistic model yields a precession period of $P=290\pm30$\,Myr, consistent with geodetic precession in a supermassive black hole binary (SMBHB) with a binary separation of less than 1.1\,pc. We expect that deep radio continuum surveys in the near future will uncover many more such systems, helping to further strengthen the connection between jet precession and SMBHBs, and the latter's implications for the gravitational-wave background detected with pulsar timing arrays.  

Galaxy bias of radio AGN and star-forming galaxies from MIGHTEE and CMB lensing  
Joel Hamlett, Catherine L. Hale, Matt J. Jarvis, David Alonso, S. Lyla Jung, Natalia Stylianou, Rohan G. Varadaraj  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23567v1  
Published: 2026-10-16T14:52:09Z  
Radio galaxies play a significant role in galaxy evolution through feedback mechanisms, making it important to characterize the environments in which they reside. We measure the cross-correlation between the positions of radio sources in the MIGHTEE survey and cosmic microwave background lensing measurements from the Atacama Cosmology Telescope over $20\ {\rm deg}^2$ to flux densities $S_{\rm 1.4\,GHz}&gt;40\ {\rm μJy}$, detecting the signal at $\sim4σ$ significance. By combining this with auto-correlation measurements of low- and high-redshift radio samples, we constrain the redshift evolution of the galaxy bias parameter, $b(z)$. Fitting different models for the redshift evolution of the bias, we find that both a model inversely proportional to the linear growth factor, $b(z) = (1.63^{+0.19}_{-0.22})/D(z)$, and a model where bias grows linearly with redshift, $b(z) = (0.49^{+0.44}_{-0.32}) + (1.62^{+0.37}_{-0.51})z$, provide comparably good fits to the data, with the current measurements unable to distinguish between them. Using a radio luminosity function, we estimate the fraction of sources that are active galactic nuclei (AGNs) or star-forming galaxies (SFGs) as a function of redshift, and model the individual contributions of AGNs and SFGs to the total bias. This approach allows us to measure the bias of AGNs and SFGs without individual source classifications. Though no single model is favoured significantly, the best-fitting model gives $b_{\rm AGN}(z) = (2.60^{+0.65}_{-0.91})/D(z)$ compared to $b_{\rm SFG}(z) = (0.52^{+0.47}_{-0.34})/D(z)$. Incorporating SFG bias measurements from previous work as a prior tightens these constraints to $b_{\rm AGN}(z) = (1.96^{+0.37}_{-0.40})/D(z)$ and $b_{\rm SFG}(z) = (0.90^{+0.06}_{-0.06})/D(z)$.  

Exploring Cosmic Rays from Gravity-Limited Acceleration at Supermassive Black Holes  
Elena Manao, Elisa Resconi, Frank M. Rieger, Paolo Padovani, Gia Dvali  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23393v1  
Published: 2026-10-16T12:51:28Z  
We show that horizon-scale magnetic fields around supermassive black holes (SMBHs), only limited by gravity, yield a universal, model-independent ceiling on electromagnetic energy density. For rotating SMBHs, this field enables particle acceleration up to $\sim\!10^{27}$\,eV set solely by fundamental constants. We identify a parameter space in accretion rate and magnetic field strength in which quiescent nuclei simultaneously satisfy the transparency conditions for Ultra-High-Energy Cosmic Rays (UHECR) escape and the field requirements for reaching Ultra-High Energies (UHE), naturally complementing photon-rich Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN), which favor neutrino production but suppress UHECR escape. We observe that, when including curvature energy losses, the energy of the primaries scales weakly as $E_{\rm curv}\!\propto\!M^{1/4}$ and allows to achieve UHE across different compositions and SMBHs masses ($10^6$--$10^{10}\,M_\odot$), without requiring extreme spins or super-Eddington accretion. A cross-correlation of the Pierre Auger Observatory (PAO) public data with galaxies hosting reliably measured SMBH masses, separating AGN from quiescent hosts, reveals marginal local excesses ($\lesssim\!2.8σ$) consistent with isotropy after correcting for the look-elsewhere effect. The framework provides a direct link between black-hole physics and the maximum energies of cosmic particles, and defines concrete observational tests for next-generation UHECR datasets.  

Comparing Infrared &amp; X-ray-selected Active Galactic Nuclei host galaxy star formation rates to the galaxy main sequence  
Dustin T. Mason, James R. Mullaney, Liam P. Grimmett  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23349v1  
Published: 2026-10-16T11:55:45Z  
A key method of exploring the relationship between active galactic nuclei (AGN) and their host galaxies is to determine the astrophysical properties of galaxies that host AGN, and compare them to those that do not. Star formation rates are believed to be strongly related to nuclear activity, both indirectly via their common fuel supply, and directly via the energy injected by the AGN either suppressing or enhancing star formation. Strong observational evidence of a direct link between nuclear activity and in-situ star formation has proven elusive, likely in part due to the very different timescales of nuclear activity and episodes of star formation. To overcome these challenges, some studies have demonstrated that evidence of the aforementioned link may be found in the distributions of AGN power and star formation rates. While previous attempts to determine these distributions have largely focused on X-ray-selected samples of AGN, in this study we also attempt to determine the star formation rate distributions of infrared-selected AGN. The results vary between our samples, but at low redshift (0.8 &lt; z &lt; 1.2) we find that more powerful X-ray-selected AGN are more likely to occur in galaxies with higher specific star formation rates, consistent with previous studies. At higher redshifts (1.0 &lt; z &lt; 1.8), this trend is reversed with higher power AGN tending to occur in galaxies with lower specific star formation rates. By contrast, infrared-selected galaxies at 1.0 &lt; z &lt; 1.8 show no evidence of a changing star formation rate distribution with AGN power.

This study combines multi-wavelength continuum data with optical spectral properties for a far-infrared (FIR)-selected sample of Type 1 active galactic nuclei (AGNs) from AKARI data. We explore the relation between AGN physics and concomitant star formation processes using empirical correlations of optical and multiwavelength data. We measured spectral parameters including full widths at half maximum (FWHMs) and velocity shifts of broad and narrow optical emission lines, as well as continuum luminosities from GALEX FUV to AKARI 160 microns. The sample contains 88 AGNs observed at both optical and FIR wavelengths. About 50% are Seyfert 1s with both broad H$β$ and H$α$ lines; almost all remaining objects are S1.9s with only broad H$α$. However, most objects fall into Population A along the quasar EV1 main sequence, where narrower broad H$β$ profiles and FIR excess emission are commonly linked to higher Eddington ratios.

URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23081v1  
Published: 2026-10-16T07:52:13Z  
We audit an automated relativistic-reflection pipeline that fitted one XMM-Newton EPIC-pn and NuSTAR epoch for each of 57 AGN with relxillCp+xillverCp, and repeat the analysis with its failures corrected. The pipeline's apparent constraints were not controlled: Small Window pn backgrounds were never subtracted, the nearest-in-time pairing selected mismatched or nearly empty spectra, single-start fits often stopped short of the minimum, and most fits were inadequate or at a parameter limit. Simulations show that, with a correct model, the 90% sets contain the true spin in 82--92% of cases without drifting edge-on, but at high inclination one low-spin simulation in six yields a false lower bound. Alberto Domínguez  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23028v1  
Published: 2026-10-16T07:04:41Z  
Two stacking analyses with the Fermi Large Area Telescope (LAT) detected GeV emission from radio-quiet Seyferts, one selected by an X-ray ultra-fast outflow (UFO) and one hard-X-ray-bright, with similar luminosities attributed to different mechanisms: wind shocks and the accretion-disk corona. We investigate how efficiently Seyfert outflows convert mechanical power into GeV photons, whether the two detections require distinct mechanisms, and how Seyferts compare with low-ionization nuclear emission-line regions (LINERs). Without new Fermi-LAT data reduction, we homogenize published measurements to 0.1-100 GeV, place local mechanical accelerators on one L_gamma-Edot diagram, and express eta = L_gamma/Edot as a fraction rho of the complete-calorimetry efficiency eta_max ~ 2.5%, inverting rho for the ambient density required by pp losses. Winds from Seyfert active galactic nuclei (AGN) have rho = 0.020 (+0.018/-0.011) for acceleration efficiency 0.1, a factor of ~13.5 below starburst superwinds (rho = 0.26 (+0.37/-0.16)). The difference exceeds a factor of three with probability 0.95 and is independent of acceleration parameters common to both classes; it is conditional on the wind kinetic powers, collapsing only if every wind sits at its minimum launch radius. The implied densities, ~10 and ~100 cm^-3, are consistency values: density, shock radius and speed are degenerate. The two stacking detections agree within a factor of 1.75 in gamma-ray output per unit Eddington luminosity, but the 95% interval, 0.26-24, is too wide to distinguish them. A third stack, of 186 low-luminosity AGN, extends the comparison 1.5 dex lower, ordered by AGN power. GeV detectability of non-jetted AGN is set by the product of mechanical power and calorimetric fraction. LINER outflows fail on power; for the adopted wind energetics, Seyfert UFO winds fail on calorimetry; starburst superwinds succeed on both.  

Resolving the AGN Feeding and Feedback Cycle with ALMA2040  
Ilaria Ruffa, Francesco Salvestrini, Almudena Alonso Herrero, Anelise Audibert, Elena Bertola, Viviana Casasola, Roberto Decarli, Daniel Espada, Umberto Maio, Dieu D. Nguyen, Mamta Pandey-Pommier, Miguel Pereira-Santaella, Francesca Pozzi, Jahang Prathap, Sabine Thater, Maria Vittoria Zanchettin  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22449v1  
Published: 2026-10-15T15:32:38Z  
Understanding how supermassive black holes (SMBHs) grow and regulate the growth of their host galaxies requires tracing the cold molecular interstellar medium (ISM) throughout the full AGN feeding-feedback cycle. Molecular gas constitutes both the primary fuel for star formation and black-hole accretion and one of the ISM phases most directly affected by AGN-driven radiation, winds, and radio jets. Over the past decade, ALMA has resolved circumnuclear molecular discs, rings, spirals, tori, and jet-driven outflows on scales of tens of parsecs in nearby AGN. These studies have demonstrated that combining spatially resolved gas kinematics with multi-line diagnostics is essential for disentangling the physical processes underlying the observed gas conditions. However, our current picture remains largely based on a limited number of exceptionally nearby systems. Most local AGN have instead been observed with ALMA in a single transition at moderate or low angular resolution, preventing a systematic characterisation of both the dynamics and physical state of the gas. Moreover, existing studies have largely overlooked kinetic-mode AGN hosted by massive early-type galaxies, despite their central importance for understanding maintenance-mode feedback. In this White Paper, we argue that ALMA2040 should transform these detailed case studies into a statistical framework. A $\sim10\times$ improvement in line sensitivity relative to the current ALMA, together with fast mosaicking, ultra-wide instantaneous bandwidth, and longer baselines (resolving up to $\sim9$ mas at 230 GHz), will enable homogeneous, multi-line mapping of the molecular ISM on giant molecular clouds scales across statistically significant samples of both radiative- and kinetic-mode AGN out to $z\lesssim0.1$.
