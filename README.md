AGN Feedback Turbulence in Cool-core Clusters: Evidence from XRISM  
Congyao Zhang, Eugene Churazov, Irina Zhuravleva, Hannah McCall, Annie Heinrich, William R. Forman, Ildar Khabibullin, Michal Zajaček, Norbert Werner  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12845v1  
Published: 2026-11-09T18:39:54Z  
The XRISM X-ray Observatory has revealed evidence for a "double-cascade" scenario in the Perseus cluster, in which active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback and mergers drive turbulence over distinct radial ranges and spatial scales. Whether this gas kinematic feature is common among cool-core clusters remains unclear. Here, we revisit XRISM observations of A1795 and A2029 to investigate the signatures of AGN feedback and their contribution to the gas velocity fields. In A1795, AGN feedback dominates the velocity dispersion in the core, similar to Perseus, and may provide turbulent heating at a rate comparable to radiative cooling. In contrast, we obtain only an upper limit on the AGN feedback contribution in A2029, where merger-driven motions dominate. Tailored simulations indicate that an off-axis minor merger $\simeq1.6$ Gyr ago displaced the central low-entropy gas in A2029, potentially suppressing AGN activity. This gas is now returning to the center, possibly facilitating the re-establishment of AGN feedback. We propose an evolutionary view of the double-cascade scenario, in which merger-driven motions dominate during and shortly after significant mergers, while feedback-driven turbulence re-emerges in cluster cores as systems relax. This framework provides a basis for interpreting XRISM kinematic measurements of (weak) cool-core clusters. Our findings highlight the importance of spatially resolved gas kinematics and detailed modeling for identifying double-cascade features and disentangling the contributions of AGN feedback and mergers.  

The Edge of the Warm Corona in Active Galactic Nuclei  
Parikshit Partha Biswas, Agata Różańska, Debora Lančová, Dominik Gronkiewicz, Rafał Wojaczyński, Andrzej Zdziarski, Stefano Bianchi  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12595v1  
Published: 2026-11-09T15:44:16Z  
A model of warm, dissipative corona on top of an accretion disk is investigated, to explain the soft X-ray excess (SXE) emission observed in active galactic nuclei (AGN). The final ray-traced spectrum, computed through photoionization code with Comptonization, accounts for both: emitted and reflected components. As a result, the strength of the SXE depends on the radial extend of the warm corona, i.e., its outer edge. We show that the radial transition from warm corona to disk atmosphere is governed by line cooling. By moving away from a central black hole Compton cooling is gradually replaced by line cooling, and the temperature drops sharply in vertical direction at a narrow iron ionization front, where iron ions Fe \textsc{xxv}--Fe \textsc{xxvi} recombine to Fe \textsc{xvii}. Notably, this transition takes place at a particular value of internal heating $\approx 1.6 \times 10^{-23}$ erg cm$^{3}$ s$^{-1}$, regardless of the Eddington ratio. We show, that at the given metal abundance, the SXE emitting area is larger for a higher accretion rate, in agreement with recent findings in changing look AGN. The overall emission spectrum from a warm corona is featureless, and the only iron K$_α$ line comes from a reflection. The line is stronger and more gravitationally distorted for a lower accretion rate, which is a direct result of the size of the warm corona. Furthermore, by increasing the iron abundance, the iron ionization front moves closer towards black hole, and a stronger iron line complex is emitted locally.  

Probing faint radio activity from cores to filaments in two $z=2.24$ protoclusters with uGMRT  
Yuheng Zhang, Xianzhong Zheng, Fangxia An, Tao Wang, Qinghua Tan, Dongdong Shi, Hao Peng  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11107v1  
Published: 2026-11-08T03:39:40Z  
We present deep 1.26 GHz radio continuum observations of the two $z=2.24$ protoclusters BOSS1244 and BOSS1542 with the upgraded Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope (uGMRT) to investigate their members' radio emission and its possible environmental dependence. Among 187 and 105 H$α$ emitter (HAE) members in BOSS1244 and BOSS1542, we identify ten and two robust radio counterparts, respectively, together with five potential counterparts from forced photometry. Two HAEs and the massive quiescent galaxy QG1 in BOSS1244 show strong radio excesses relative to their H$α$-based star formation rates (SFRs) and are identified as strong radio-excess AGN candidates. Median stacking of the remaining 173 and 102 radio-undetected HAE members yields positive signals at $2.8σ$ and $3.2σ$ in BOSS1244 and BOSS1542, respectively. The stacked radio luminosities are consistent with those expected from the median H$α$-based SFRs, indicating that star formation can account for the average faint radio emission. After excluding the robust radio counterparts, we divided the HAE members by the projected distance from QG1 in BOSS1244 and from the filament spine in BOSS1542. The largest stacked radio luminosities are found toward the denser regions in both systems, but the differences are not statistically significant, and stellar mass shows no significant dependence on environmental distance. These results identify strong radio-excess AGN candidates among the individually detected members, while the faint HAE population is on average consistent with radio emission from star formation. The similar environmental ordering is suggestive but remains tentative. Deeper radio observations at multiple frequencies and higher angular resolution are required to determine whether this apparent trend reflects weak AGN activity or variations in radio emission associated with star formation.
