The 943 MHz radio-SFR relation of nearby galaxies observed with ASKAP  
Shengtao Wang, Xiaohui Sun, Jiangtao Li, Chao-Wei Tsai, Jianghui Xu  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41895v1  
Published: 2026-10-30T15:35:12Z  
We investigate the integrated 943 MHz radio-infrared and radio-star-formation-rate (SFR) relations of 875 nearby galaxies within $D&lt;50$ Mpc using public Australian SKA Pathfinder (ASKAP) observations. After excluding galaxies with identified or candidate AGN activity, 594 galaxies remain in the AGN-cleaned sample. We used Bayesian errors-in-variables models that account for measurement uncertainties, intrinsic scatter, and correlated distance errors. The AGN-cleaned radio-infrared relation is nearly linear, $L_{ν,943}\propto L_{ν,\mathrm{W4}}^{1.09\pm0.02}$, with an orthogonal intrinsic scatter of $0.21\pm0.01$ dex, and is tighter than the AGN-host relation. For 592 AGN-cleaned galaxies, the radio-SFR relation is approximately linear, $L_{ν,943}\propto{\rm SFR}^{1.02\pm0.02}$, with an intrinsic scatter of $0.25\pm0.01$ dex. Semi-calorimetric models reveal positive secondary dependences on stellar mass, $γ=0.09\pm0.03$, and rotation velocity, $δ=0.24\pm0.08$. The consistency between these two secondary trends suggests that the galaxy gravitational potential may weakly regulate CRE confinement and synchrotron-radiation efficiency. Our results provide an integrated 943 MHz radio-SFR calibration for nearby galaxies and show that, although radio luminosity scales nearly proportionally with SFR on average, weak secondary dependences on galaxy properties remain.  

The Broad-Line Region Size-Luminosity Relation in AGN Constrained by the Viewing Angle with the FRADO Model  
Junhee Myong, Jong-Hak Woo, Mohammad-Hassan Naddaf, Bożena Czerny, Jimin Kim  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41594v1  
Published: 2026-10-30T11:28:32Z  
We investigate the H$β$ broad-line region (BLR) size-luminosity ($R_{\rm BLR}$-$L_{5100}$) relation of active galactic nuclei (AGN) using a Bayesian viewing angle inference framework based on the Failed Radiatively Accelerated Dusty Outflow (FRADO) model. First, we update the FRADO model by introducing a phase factor to account for the anisotropic visibility of the illuminated cloud surface. Second, we construct a Bayesian framework to constrain the viewing angle by matching the FRADO-based $R_{\rm BLR}$ to the measured H$β$ lags of 151 AGN from Wang &amp; Woo (2024). We successfully reproduce the slope and intrinsic scatter of the observed $R_{\rm BLR}$-$L_{5100}$ relation, finding that the inferred viewing angles are broadly consistent with the estimates from dynamical modeling of velocity-resolved reverberation data, albeit with substantial scatter. As the observed H$β$ time lag and line velocity depend on the viewing angle, the black hole mass ($M_{\rm BH}$) calibrated from our work is typically larger than the reverberation-mapping estimates using an average virial factor. Our results provide a physically motivated interpretation of the $R_{\rm BLR}$-$L_{5100}$ relation and the systematic uncertainties in reverberation and single-epoch $M_{\rm BH}$ estimates.  

Co-evolution of AGN and Host Galaxies across Cosmic Time Using JWST data  
Priyanka Jalan, Tomotsugu Goto, Tetsuya Hashimoto, Angel Rodriguez Barbosa, Chih-Teng Ling, Ece Kilerci, Terry Long Phan, Srivardini Ayyappan  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41584v1  
Published: 2026-10-30T11:20:29Z  
Understanding the complex co-evolution of active galactic nuclei (AGN) and their host galaxies across cosmic time remains a fundamental challenge, particularly in identifying how the physical drivers of galaxy diversity evolve from the early Universe to the present day. To understand this, we use a multi-wavelength JWST catalog of 2,709 sources cross-matched from the SMILES and JWST Advanced Deep Extragalactic Survey (JADES) surveys, spanning lookback times from 0.8 to 12.9 Gyr ($0.05 &lt;z &lt;6$). Using Pearson correlation matrix, we find AGN luminosity ($L_{\mathrm{AGN}}$) strongly correlates ($\sim0.8$) with star formation rate (SFR) and dust luminosity ($L_{\mathrm{dust}}$), weakly correlates ($\sim0.5$) with stellar mass ($M_\star$) and anti-correlates ($\sim -0.4$) with star formation history (SFH) duration and stellar age (Age$_\star$). However, the apparent anti-correlation observed between galaxy age and AGN luminosity is driven by cosmic time rather than by a direct physical connection. Using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), we find that galaxy diversity is fundamentally governed by two principal components: AGN and galaxy activity, and stellar age. At higher redshifts, AGN accretion, star formation, and dust emission are tightly synchronized, reflecting gas-rich conditions in the early Universe. In contrast, the present Universe exhibits a clear bimodal structure in host-dominated systems ($f_{\mathrm{AGN}} \leq 0.2$), separating quiescent and star-forming galaxies. Our results demonstrate that AGN luminosity is primarily governed by the host galaxy's current cold-gas supply, of which recent star formation is a close observational tracer, with galaxy mass setting the overall potential. This highlights a co-evolution scenario in which black hole growth and star formation are tightly linked, particularly at early cosmic epochs.  

From Quantum Field Fluctuations to High-Redshift AGN: Semiclassical Origins of Halo Mass Function Constraints in the JWST Era  
Elan Yogeswaren, Amir-Homayoon Najmi, Robert K Schaefer  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40846v1  
Published: 2026-10-29T18:28:36Z  
We develop a continuous framework connecting quantum field fluctuations in the early Universe to the abundance and observable properties of galaxies and active galactic nuclei (AGN) at cosmic dawn. Beginning with quantum fields in curved Friedmann Lemaitre Robertson Walker spacetime, we follow inflationary vacuum fluctuations into the primordial curvature spectrum, linear matter power spectrum, mass variance $σ(MHz)$, mass peak height $ν(MHz)$, and the statistics of nonlinear halo formation. The Press-Schechter and Sheth-Tormen formalisms provide an analytic description of halo abundance and its dependence on peak rarity. A complementary high redshift halo mass function calibrated with the GUREFT simulations of Yung et al. (2024) provides a numerical description specifically constructed for the cosmic dawn regime probed by JWST. The comparison between these analytic and simulation calibrated prescriptions provides a direct measure of the sensitivity of predicted halo populations to the observations. We separate this cosmological supply of halos from the astrophysical and observational mappings that produce stars, ultraviolet luminosities, and active galactic nuclei. Controlled numerical experiments show that a percent level perturbation in the primordial fluctuation amplitude produces only a modest change in $σ$ and $ν$, but can strongly amplify the exponentially suppressed high $ν$ tail of the Halo Mass Function. This sensitivity increases toward high halo mass and high redshift, identifying rare cosmic dawn populations as potentially powerful probes of primordial statistics while simultaneously exposing the importance of uncertainties in halo abundance. High redshift AGN population statistics and the Ultraviolet Luminosity Function provide additional constraints on the primordial power spectrum and dynamics of primordial inflation theory.  

How to feed your dragon: the role of turbulence in AGN activity  
Olga Borodina, Jake S. Bennett, Yueying Ni, Rainer Weinberger, Michaela Hirschmann, Boon Kiat Oh, Lars Hernquist  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40788v1  
Published: 2026-10-29T18:00:14Z  
Active galactic nuclei (AGN) are thought to be important drivers of galaxy evolution. However, the way AGN jets couple their energy to the interstellar medium (ISM) remains poorly understood. Recent observations have revealed $\sim$kpc-scale jets in galaxies of different morphological types. Here, we use idealized turbulent box simulations with accreting black holes to investigate how jet-ISM interactions and feedback shape the evolution of jets. We show that a turbulent ISM is a key ingredient in both jet launching and jet propagation. Without turbulence, jet feedback initially shuts off the cold gas supply to the black hole and the system enters a steady state with jet power $\leq 10^{39}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}}$ for a $10^{6}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$ black hole. In a turbulent medium, jets fill voids with hot diffuse material which the turbulence then carries away, replenishing the black hole's fuel supply. However, above a critical jet efficiency factor, the jet begins to significantly deplete its fuel supply and enters a self-limiting accretion regime. This critical factor depends on the ISM Mach number, with lower turbulence yielding a lower threshold. Moreover, while the jet power depends on the ambient gas density in the vicinity of the black hole, we find that, at fixed turbulent structure, the morphology and extent of jet propagation are largely insensitive to the average background density; turbulence in the ISM dominates over mean density in setting how far jets propagate, although denser media experience higher total energy injection and stronger heating.  

Cosmic merger rates and population evolution of binary black hole mergers in AGN disks  
Maria Paola Vaccaro, Alessandro Trinca, Michela Mapelli, Lumen Boco, Raffaella Schneider, Rosa Valiante, Dominika Wylezalek  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40787v1  
Published: 2026-10-29T18:00:14Z  
Active galactic nuclei (AGN) disks are promising sites for hierarchical binary black hole (BBH) mergers, which next-generation gravitational-wave detectors will probe out to high redshift. Predicting their contribution requires linking BBH evolution in AGN disks to the poorly constrained high-redshift evolution of active supermassive black holes (SMBHs). We introduce ARISE (AGN Rates and Intrinsic Stellar-BH Evolution), a framework that embeds semi-analytical models of BBH evolution in AGN disks into a cosmological description of the AGN number density, SMBH mass function, Eddington-ratio distribution, and nuclear metallicity. We consider a conservative AGN-abundance model anchored to observed luminosity functions, as well as Eddington-limited and super-Eddington scenarios based on the CAT cosmological model, compatible with JWST observations of high-redshift AGNs. The merger-rate density is set primarily by the disk viscosity parameter $α$: at $z = 0.2$, we find a rate of $12$--$37$ Gpc$^{-3}$ yr$^{-1}$ for $α= 0.01$ and $0.5$--$6$ Gpc$^{-3}$ yr$^{-1}$ for $α= 0.1$. The redshift evolution depends strongly on how SMBHs grow. The rate density peaks at $z \simeq 1.8$, $3.3$, and $3.9$ ($1.5$, $3.6$, and $3.9$) for $α=0.01$ ($0.1$) in the conservative, Eddington-limited, and super-Eddington models, respectively, and in the super-Eddington model it stays nearly flat out to $z \simeq 10$. For $α= 0.1$, AGN disks account for $\sim 2$--$40\%$ of the LVK-inferred merger rate, consistent with current upper limits from GW--AGN associations. For $α= 0.01$, they match or exceed it. The Einstein Telescope (ET) should detect $\sim 10^{5}$ ($\sim 10^{4}$) AGN-disk mergers per year for $α= 0.01$ ($0.1$). ET will test the AGN channel at redshifts where the AGN population models differ most strongly, providing independent constraints on the growth of early SMBHs.
