Toward the one- and two-loop EFTofLSS for Simons Observatory CMB lensing  
Evan Saraivanov, Henrique Rubira, Vivian Miranda  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03556v1  
Published: 2026-11-02T18:18:25Z  
Cosmic microwave background (CMB) lensing observations are becoming sensitive to non-linear structure formation and baryonic feedback scenarios. The effective field theory of large-scale structure (EFTofLSS) provides a systematic description of matter clustering in the mildly non-linear regime, accounting for baryonic effects through counterterms and offering a consistent framework for cross-correlations with other observables. We perform a Fisher forecast for the joint Simons Observatory temperature, polarization, and CMB lensing likelihood using noiseless data vectors with different baryonic feedback scenarios, modelling non-linear structure formation using the one- and two-loop EFTofLSS. We find that, with a single additional parameter, obtained through a principal-component analysis of the EFTofLSS counterterms, both the one- and two-loop EFTofLSS provide a good fit across the baryonic feedback scenarios considered. This parameter is closely aligned with the effective AGN heating temperature $T_{\rm AGN}$. Both loop orders reduce cosmological parameter biases, with only a small loss in marginalized precision at fixed scale cuts. We find that the one-loop EFTofLSS yields unbiased estimates over a range of scales similar to the dark-matter-only model, while the two-loop EFTofLSS extends this range to smaller scales and accommodates different feedback scenarios more robustly. When each model is evaluated at the smallest scales for which its cosmological parameter estimates remain unbiased, the two-loop EFTofLSS yields stronger constraints than the dark-matter-only model.

URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03546v1  
Published: 2026-11-02T18:08:39Z  
We present 136 hr of 340 MHz observations of the Hubble Ultra Deep Field (HUDF) conducted with the VLA Low-band Ionosphere and Transient Experiment (VLITE), collected commensally with higher-frequency Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA) observations. Our deepest image combines 20 hr of data, covers 4 square degrees, and achieves an angular resolution of 8.8 arcsec x 3.8 arcsec with an rms sensitivity of 158 microJy per beam at the field center. We catalog 671 radio sources detected above 5 sigma significance. Correcting for incompleteness using injection-recovery simulations with a model radio continuum population, we show that our source counts recover the sub-mJy star-forming galaxy population at abundances consistent with other deep fields at comparable frequencies. The data span 45 observing days across two VLA A-configuration semesters separated by 1.3 years, providing a search for variability on ~1-year timescales at sub-mJy flux densities. We identify eleven variable sources with flux densities of 1-100 mJy and modulation indices of 6-40%, six of which exhibit peaked radio spectra consistent with compact AGN. Their variability amplitudes are consistent with refractive interstellar scintillation (RISS) predicted for this line of sight, though three sources with modulation indices exceeding 30% likely require an intrinsic contribution. One source, subsequently confirmed as a compact symmetric object through VLBA follow-up observations, demonstrates the potential of VLITE for identifying populations of young radio AGN through commensal low-frequency variability searches.

Measuring BLR size and black hole mass of jetted quasars with strong radio emission is challenging due to the contribution from non-thermal synchrotron radiation from the jet in the optical continuum. Here, we investigate the optical continuum emission in two flat-spectrum radio quasars (FSRQs), PKS~0736+017 and PKS~1510-089, by separating the thermal emission from the accretion disk and the non-thermal synchrotron emission from the jet using their distinct polarization signatures. URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02445v1  
Published: 2026-11-02T03:08:33Z  
Tidal disruption events (TDEs) can reach super-Eddington luminosities near their peak. While their optical/UV emission may arise from multiple processes, X-rays are more likely to be produced by the innermost accretion flow close to the black hole, analogous to super-Eddington flows in stellar-mass X-ray binaries (XRBs) and active galactic nuclei (AGNs). Super-Eddington AGNs typically show disc-dominated spectra, with weak and steep coronae, while super-Eddington XRBs show a wider variety of spectral shapes. Here we explore disc-corona coupling in TDEs by developing a super-Eddington ($\dot{m}&gt;1$: slim) disc model in which magnetic reconnection transfers some of the accretion power from the disc into a hot Comptonizing corona. We test the model on the well-monitored X-ray TDE AT2021ehb, and can broadly fit the brightest X-ray spectra with a black-hole mass $M_{\rm BH}\sim10^6$--$10^7\,M_\odot$ at $\dot m\sim 1$, but need variable magnetic coupling to the disc to match the large observed fast changes in coronal contribution. AGN at similarly high luminosity do not show such variable coronae, but the transient XRB outbursts do, suggesting that it may be a common feature of rapidly changing, non-steady accretion flows. As the TDE declines to $\dot{m}\sim 0.01$, the source hardens, but never goes below $Γ\sim 2$, unlike both AGN and XRB at these luminosities. Thus the X-ray spectral evolution in this TDE does not resemble either XRB or AGN in detail, likely pointing to the very different, highly variable nature of the accretion flow in a tidal disruption event.

Environmental density correlates with galaxy properties in the local Universe, but how and when these trends emerge is unclear: at cosmic noon, large-scale structures are still assembling and galaxies quench rapidly for debated reasons. We study how the formation and quenching histories of massive galaxies at $1.7&lt;z&lt;3.5$ depend on environment, using two independent tracers: galaxy number density ($δ_\#$) from COSMOS-Web and intergalactic neutral hydrogen ($δ_{HI}$, cool gas) from LATIS Ly$α$ tomography. Galaxy counts alone cannot capture the environment at cosmic noon: jointly mapping galaxies and cool gas is crucial to understand how environment regulates gas accretion, star formation, and quenching.
