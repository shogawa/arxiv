Centaurus A (NGC 5128) is the nearest radio galaxy (d = 3.8 Mpc), so individual X-ray point sources can be resolved against its giant radio lobes. Reverberation mapping of active galactic nuclei uses the time taken for variability to travel through the system as a measure of system size, with this size being used to inform estimates of the mass of the central black hole, a technique that has graduated to an industrial-scale effort in recent decades. Such large target-count, multi-year surveys have been consistently undercut by poor light curve quality and the aliasing problems of seasonal observations. Here, we present a new end-to-end Bayesian framework for fitting lags at scale in large surveys, simultaneous with the fitting of population models such as the radius-luminosity relation, and formulated in a numerically tractable way. We present new ALMA ${}^{13}$CO(2-1) observations of the central galaxy in the cool-core cluster Abell 1795, targeting the origin of extended molecular filaments. The filaments extend for 5--7 kpc and are closely drawn up around large radio bubbles inflated by jets from the central active galactic nucleus (AGN). Smooth velocity gradients along their lengths are consistent with entrained flows lifted by the expanding bubbles. Here we detect the northern molecular filament in the low-opacity molecular tracer ${}^{13}$CO(2-1), which can provide a more reliable measure of the molecular gas mass and conditions than ${}^{12}$CO. Stars may become embedded in active galactic nucleus (AGN) accretion disks and eventually explode as supernovae (SNe), whose detection would provide direct evidence for the existence and evolution of stars in AGN disks. We investigate the electromagnetic emission of such disk SNe using an analytic shock dynamics model coupled with a one-zone radiation model. Applying this framework to four progenitor types over a range of AGN black hole masses and Eddington ratios, we find that disk SNe around lower-mass black holes (BHs) or at lower Eddington ratios are more readily detectable against the underlying AGN emission and can remain detectable over a broader range of electromagnetic bands, from the infrared to soft X-rays. We therefore further compare disk SNe with isolated SNe with and without CSM interaction and find substantial degeneracies in their macroscopic observables.
