Symbolic Regression Diagnostics of 1D Lyman Alpha Forest Emulator  
Aidan Behmer, Ming-Feng Ho, Camille Avestruz, Yanhui Yang, Simeon Bird  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31450v1  
Published: 2026-11-23T19:25:06Z  
We use symbolic regression to diagnose the parameter dependence of a black-box multi-fidelity emulator of the Lyman-alpha forest 1D flux power spectrum. Symbolic regression is appealing in its ability to reduce complex parameter spaces, thereby reducing compute time and offering potential interpretability in parameter inter-dependencies. Starting from a pretrained Gaussian-process (GP) emulator that connects low- and high-resolution PRIYA simulations, we search for explicit equations describing the dependence of the power spectrum on resolution and each cosmological and astrophysical parameter. We find that symbolic regression can recover equations that reproduce the emulator values well, but accurate predictions do not guarantee accurate parameter derivatives, making such equations unreliable for downstream applications such as Fisher forecasts. We therefore introduce a derivative-faithfulness diagnostic that directly compares the parameter gradient of each symbolic equation to the GP, and explore a derivative-matching (Sobolev) loss to improve this agreement. At z = 3.6, the Sobolev objective yields derivative-faithful equations for 9 of the 11 PRIYA parameters constraining the Lyman-alpha forest, with the approach only ineffective on h and AGN, whose 1D power spectrum imprints are weak or degenerate. Redefining the training objective with the Sobolev loss is a more robust and computationally efficient approach to gradient-matching repair than increasing model complexity. Our methods present potential mitigation strategies for failure modes of PySR, enabling downstream Fisher forecasts in some of these cases. Further, we document limitations potentially relevant to future work bridging symbolic regression with emulators. Our results demonstrate symbolic regression as an emulator diagnostic tool, with particular utility for low-dimensional or weakly coupled parameter dependencies.  

Probing the X-Ray Structure of the $z = 2.38$ Strongly Lensed Quasar J1721+8842, the First Einstein Zig-Zag Lens, Down to $140\ \mathrm{pc}$  
Charlotte A. Eades, Júlia M. Sisk-Reynés, Daniel A. Schwartz, Anna Barnacka  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31386v1  
Published: 2026-11-23T19:00:09Z  
Sub-kiloparsec X-ray structures in active galactic nuclei (AGN), including outflows, jets, and dual AGN, provide key insights into their evolution and influence on host galaxies. In the local universe, Chandra X-ray Observatory's state-of-the-art $0.5''$ angular resolution has resolved these structures, but at $z &gt; 0.1$, milliarcsecond resolution is required to spatially resolve sub-kpc scales. We leverage the spatial amplification by strong gravitational lensing to investigate the X-ray structure of J1721+8842, a $z=2.38$ quasar lensed into six images. To reconstruct the optical emission position, we model the lensing mass distribution using the six quasar images, five detected by Gaia Data Release 3 (A-E) and one by HST (F). Our model reproduces the five Gaia image positions to milliarcsecond precision. To locate the X-ray emission relative to the optical, we combine ray-trace simulations of archival Chandra observations with the lens model image position predictions using a double maximum-likelihood analysis. We constrain the X-ray centroid within a $0.017''\times0.004''\ (139\times30\ \mathrm{pc})$ ellipse centered $0.005''\ (44\ \mathrm{pc})$ away from the optical source position. Source-plane reconstructions of the soft-X-ray and hard-X-ray to optical sources demonstrate these emission regions are cospatial, allowing the optical source at $1σ$ confidence. These results indicate no evidence for kpc-scale X-ray jets, outflows, or dual AGN with projected separations exceeding $140\ \mathrm{pc}$. This method improves Chandra's effective angular resolution by a factor of 30 at cosmological distances, enabling studies of sub-kpc AGN X-ray structure at cosmic noon.  

AGN feedback in the multiphase inner intracluster medium of a cosmological super-resolved galaxy cluster  
Marine Prunier, Rahul Ramesh, Dylan Nelson, Annalisa Pillepich, Julie Hlavacek-Larrondo, Katrin Lehle, Joey Braspenning  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31367v1  
Published: 2026-11-23T19:00:02Z  
The intracluster medium (ICM) of galaxy cluster cores is a multiphase environment regulated by active galactic nuclei (AGN) feedback. Extended cool gas nebulae with complex morphologies and kinematics are commonly observed in cool-core clusters, but their origin and interaction with AGN feedback remain poorly understood. We present a cosmological magnetohydrodynamical zoom-in simulation of a low-mass cool-core galaxy cluster ($M_{\mathrm{200c}}=2.3\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$), using initial conditions from TNG-Cluster and the IllustrisTNG galaxy formation model. Through super-Lagrangian refinement, we achieve a baryonic mass resolution of $2.3\times10^{4}\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$ and a spatial resolution of $\sim40\text{-}90$ pc in the cool ($10^{4}\,\mathrm{K}&lt;T&lt;10^{5.5}\,\mathrm{K}$) gas phase within the central $370$ ckpc, while retaining the full cosmological context. We investigate the properties and evolution of cool clouds and filaments and their interaction with AGN feedback at $z\leq0.35$. The cluster core hosts a rich population of dense, low-entropy filaments, including inflowing and outflowing structures and occasional rotationally supported disks. Comparing simulations with AGN kinetic feedback turned off and on, we find that AGN activity fragments and redistributes the cool gas and increases its covering fraction without destroying the cool gas reservoir. Individual outbursts drive transient increases in the velocities and velocity dispersions of both cool and hot gas, on short, $\lesssim 50$ Myr, timescales. We also identify low-entropy gas uplifted by AGN-driven outflows. While feedback globally heats the ICM and offsets cooling, it also creates conditions that may favor local thermal instabilities -- through shock compressions, mixing, turbulence, and uplift.  

XRISM view of a featureless cluster Abell 1060: Revealing the advection of a diffuse radio source by ICM motions  
Kohei Kurahara, Kazuhiro Nakazawa, Yuki Omiya, Daisuke Ito, Kosuke Nishiwaki, Andrea Botteon, Takuya Akahori, Noriko Y. Yamasaki, Liyi Gu, Kosuke Sato, Takayuki Tamura  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30001v1  
Published: 2026-11-23T04:06:21Z  
Abell 1060 is a nearby, dynamically relaxed galaxy cluster hosting the extended radio structure Flying Fox (FF) near its center. We used high-resolution XRISM/Resolve spectroscopy to measure intracluster medium (ICM) motions and velocity dispersion. Spatially resolved analysis reveals a velocity gradient of approximately 100 km/s along FF and enhanced velocity dispersion near the brightest cluster galaxy NGC 3311, suggesting turbulence generated by galaxy-ICM interactions. These results and the compact extent of FF favor fossil plasma supplied by past activity of the brightest cluster galaxy and transported by ICM motions. We also tested whether the measured turbulence could generate or maintain the nonthermal components of FF. For a velocity dispersion of $96.7^{+23.9}_{-24.6}$ km/s and an assumed turbulence scale of 50 kpc, comparable to the Resolve field of view and approximately half the cluster core radius, the turbulent energy flux is approximately $3.49 \times 10^{-28}$ erg cm$^{-3}$ s$^{-1}$. Although this flux may weakly reenergize pre-existing relativistic electrons, the acceleration timescale of approximately 2.5 Gyr exceeds the synchrotron and inverse Compton cooling timescale of approximately 210 Myr. The present turbulence is therefore insufficient to accelerate electrons to high energies. In situ turbulent reacceleration is unlikely to dominate the generation of FF, although it may help maintain fossil electrons supplied by past AGN activity. These results link ICM dynamics to the origin of nonthermal emission and support a time-evolutionary picture of particle acceleration in galaxy clusters.

Velocity-Window Dependence of the Broad H$α$ Width-Luminosity Relation in Little Red Dots  
Haoyu Wang  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29615v1  
Published: 2026-11-22T17:13:14Z  
Broad Balmer emission lines probe black hole masses and nuclear kinematics in high-redshift active galactic nuclei (AGNs). In JWST "little red dots" (LRDs), integrated broad-H$α$ widths span a remarkably narrow range at fixed line luminosity, raising the question of whether this tightness persists once outer-wing emission is included. Holding each object and its broad-H$α$ luminosity fixed, we measure the finite-window line dispersion $σ(V)$, the flux-weighted second-moment width within $\pm V$, from $V = 1500$ to $4500~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ in JWST/NIRSpec spectra and Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) Type-1 AGNs. Across 20 high signal-to-noise LRDs, the observed scatter in $\log_{10}σ(V)$ at fixed $L_{\mathrm{H}α}$ rises from 0.036 to 0.084 dex between $V = 2500$ and $4500~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, a paired increase of 0.048 dex with a 95% bootstrap interval of 0.026-0.064 dex, compared with 0.020 dex across 167 broader SDSS AGNs. Injecting synthetic profiles into the empirical noise of the same 20 spectra shows that single-Gaussian and core-plus-wing models both accommodate this rise, leaving intrinsic wing diversity, truncation, and wing noise unseparated. In the clearest dual-line spectrum, RUBIES-EGS-55604, H$β$ strengthens relative to H$α$ from line center to wing under two spectral decompositions of the same grating spectrum. Alongside integrated widths in JWST, SDSS, and 334 Euclid DR1 AGNs, these results show that the observed tightness of the H$α$ scaling depends on the velocities included in $σ(V)$, requiring mass and kinematic models to match both transition and velocity interval.
