A Comprehensive JWST Slitless Spectroscopic Census of $\sim$12,000 Emission-Line Galaxies at Redshift $0\sim9$ in COSMOS  
Shuqi Fu, Zijian Zhang, Danyang Jiang, Jie Chen, Linhua Jiang, Chenxi Bao, Yihe Wang, Ronaldo Laishram  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13920v1  
Published: 2026-10-09T17:53:18Z  
Emission-line galaxies provide key diagnostics of star formation, chemical enrichment, and AGN activity across cosmic time. We present a systematic census of emission-line sources using JWST/NIRCam wide-field slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) from the COSMOS-3D program, which covers a large contiguous area of 0.33 deg$^2$ and provides F444W grism spectroscopy over $3.9$--$5.1\ μ$m. By combining slitless spectroscopy with deep multi-band imaging data, we construct a homogeneous spectroscopic sample of 12,275 objects at redshift 0--9. The typical $3σ$ line-flux limit reaches $3.2\times10^{-18}\ {\rm erg\ s^{-1}\ cm^{-2}}$. To address source confusion inherent to slitless spectroscopy, we develop a multi-step identification framework that combines information from spectra, images, spectral energy distributions, and spatial distributions. We introduce convolutional neural network and large language model to emulate the human identification process and improve efficiency. The resulting catalog provides spectroscopic redshifts, emission-line measurements, and derived physical properties for all sources. The F444W WFSS enables the detection of multiple emission lines, including primarily [O III], H$α$, He I $\lambda10830$, [S III] $λ\lambda9531,9069$, Pa$β$, Pa$α$, Br$β$, PAH, Br$α$, allowing a continuous census of galaxies across cosmic time. We construct composite spectra in several redshift bins and detect a variety of faint spectral features that are difficult to identify in individual spectra, including higher-order hydrogen recombination lines, CO absorption bands, and [Fe II] lines. This catalog provides a key reference dataset for studies of galaxy evolution and AGN activity, and demonstrates the power of WFSS for constructing large, unbiased spectroscopic samples over wide areas. The full catalog will be publicly available online upon publication.  

High Resolution Spectro-Polarimetric Observations of the Optical Filaments surrounding NGC 1275 with ESPaDOnS  
Benjamin Vigneron, Julie Hlavacek-Larrondo, Luca Fabiani, Lison Malo, Carter Lee Rhea, Megan Donahue, Jorge Karolt Barrera Ballesteros, Hyunseop Choi, Nadine Manset  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13719v1  
Published: 2026-10-09T15:18:30Z  
We analyze archival high spectral resolution ESPaDOnS spectropolarimetric observations (R = $λ/Δλ$ = 68000) of three targeted locations within the filamentary nebula surrounding NGC 1275. We present here the analysis of the main filaments emission lines, namely: [NII]$\lambda6584$, H$α$, and [NII]$\lambda6548$, across our three pointings displaying clear detectable emission, with one also showing additional [SII]$\lambda6716$ and [SII]$\lambda6731$ emission line doublet. Using a Markov Chain Monte-Carlo (MCMC) algorithm and thanks to the exceptionally high spectral resolution of the observations, we resolve for the first time each of the emission lines within one of the pointings into a set of three emission components with a remarkable velocity dispersion contrast ($σ_1 \sim 25$ km/s, $σ_2 \sim 90$ km/s and $σ_3 \sim 208$ km/s). We also perform flux ratios of the three detected emission components, which reveal differences in electron density and ionization sources. Consequently, the ESPaDOnS observations appear to reveal for the first time the overlap of an outer quiescent filament and a central turbulent filament likely affected by the AGN activity at a distance of $\sim 6.8''$ ($\sim 2.5$ kpc) from the central BCG. Finally, ESPaDOnS polarimetric data of one of the pointing allows us to confirm an average linear polarization degree of $\sim 0.15-0.14\%$ and an angle of polarization of $5.3\pm25.7^\circ$ for the optical emission of the filaments, as well as polarization dips associated with the H$α$ emission line and its multiple emission components. We interpret these features and measurements as tentative polarization due to dust scattering.  

The Population of Type~1 Active Galactic Nuclei in Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument. I. Profile-dependent Measurements of the Low-redshift Sample  
Zhu-Heng Yao, Wei-Jian Guo, Shuo Zhai, Yu-Chang Li, Cheng Cheng, Jun-Rong Liu, Shuang Jin, Antonios Katsianis, Jing Wang, Da-Wei Xu, Jian-Yan Wei  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13218v1  
Published: 2026-10-09T08:35:26Z  
We present a spectral analysis of a large sample of low-redshift type 1 active galactic nuclei (AGNs) selected from the first data release of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI DR1). For objects at $z\le0.9$, we perform spectral fitting and ML-assisted refinement, yielding 114,754 type 1 AGNs. We use two fitting schemes that differ only in the broad-line model: the broad Balmer components are fitted with either a Lorentzian function or a sum of Gaussians. The continuum and [O III] luminosity measurements agree closely between the two schemes, whereas the Gaussians scheme generally yields larger broad-H$β$ full width at half maximum (FWHM) values, larger virial black hole masses, and lower accretion rates. The adopted broad-Balmer profile also changes the decomposition of narrow Balmer emission. Specifically, the anti-correlation between [O III]$\lambda5007$/narrow H$β$ and accretion rate is stronger under the Gaussians scheme with a Spearman rank correlation coefficient $r_{\rm s}=-0.412$, than under the Lorentzian scheme with $r_{\rm s}=-0.206$; the same tendency is observed in the narrow-line Seyfert 1 galaxies (NLS1s) subset ($r_{\rm s}=-0.435$ and $-0.259$ for the Gaussians and Lorentzian schemes, respectively), suggesting that the Gaussians scheme may provide a more self-consistent separation of the broad and narrow H$β$ components. Moreover, the consistent correlations recovered under both profile prescriptions favor a continuous type 1 accretion-state sequence, with NLS1s occupying its high-accretion end rather than constituting a distinct population.  

A Single Line-Emitter Identified in an Unbiased Search with JWST/NIRSpec: Multi-Object Coronagraphy for Slitless Spectroscopy  
Mason S. Huberty, Claudia Scarlata, Matthew J. Hayes, Alaina Henry, Danielle A. Berg, Vieri Cammelli, Richard Ellis, Anne E. Jaskot, Yu-Heng Lin, Vihang Mehta, Matilde Mingozzi, Pierluigi Monaco, Alberto Saldana-Lopez, Aayush Saxena, Jonathan C. Tan, Alice Young  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12553v1  
Published: 2026-10-08T18:00:01Z  
We report the detection of a previously unknown object with a single emission line within the Hubble Ultra Deep Field, using a new JWST NIRSpec technique called Multi-Object Coronagraphy for Slitless Spectroscopy (MOCSS) with the G140M/F100LP observing mode. The MOCSS technique involves opening almost all the shutters on the NIRSpec microshutter array, while simultaneously closing the shutters that lie on top of known low redshift objects, facilitating a wide field slitless spectroscopic search without contamination from bright objects. This observational technique has enabled the detection of a strong emission line in four different dither positions and in two position angles, and thus cannot be artificial. This line is detected at 1.60 $\rm μm$ with a line flux of $[2.5\pm 0.3]\times10^{-18} \rm erg\ s^{-1} cm^{-2}$ and a FWHM of $13.2\pm2.0$Å, and does not correspond to any known source with some of the deepest HST and JWST imaging in existence. Based upon the absence of imaging detection, we place a lower limit on the observed frame equivalent width (EW) of 7620Å on this object, which strongly limits the number of possible lines that this line can be. Given the wavelength, an EW limit that is near the ceiling of expectation, and the lack of other lines in the bandpass, this line cannot be easily explained by [OII] at $z=3.29$, [OIII] at $z=2.20$, H$α$ at $z=1.44$, and Ly$α$ at $z=12.16$. We discuss possible AGN and transient phenomena that could explain the finding.
