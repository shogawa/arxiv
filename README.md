Deep Multiwavelength Constraints on the Host Galaxies and Black-Hole Growth of Dwarf AGNs  
Wei Leong Tee, W. N. Brandt, Zhibo Yu, Fan Zou, Bojun Zhang  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24698v1  
Published: 2026-11-18T16:25:17Z  
We present a multiwavelength population study of 56 X-ray-detected dwarf galaxies ($M_* &lt; 3 \times 10^9 M_\odot$), comprising 31 active galactic nuclei (AGNs) and 25 galaxies (not satisfying AGN classification) in the Chandra Deep Fields North and South. Utilizing ultra-deep Chandra observations together with high-resolution JWST and HST imaging, we investigate the demographics, host-galaxy properties, environments, and cosmic evolution of accreting black holes in low-mass galaxies out to $z\approx4$. We find no statistically significant differences in either the local environments or non-parametric morphologies of X-ray AGNs relative to mass- and redshift-matched inactive dwarf galaxies. These results suggest that AGN activity in dwarf galaxies does not generally require ongoing major mergers or strong large-scale disturbances. Using a single power-law model for the specific accretion-rate distribution, we infer a positive cosmic evolution of dwarf-galaxy black-hole growth. Both the observed AGN fraction and the long-term average black-hole accretion rate increase toward higher redshift and show a positive stellar-mass dependence. Many dwarf AGNs exhibit weak optical and near-infrared AGN signatures, consistent with moderate accretion rates and, at high redshift, potentially enhanced obscuration. The small number of detected systems at $z&gt;2$ limits definitive conclusions, but their hard X-ray colors suggest that obscuration may play an increasingly important role in the dwarf AGN population in the early universe. Our results support a picture in which black-hole growth in dwarf galaxies proceeds through largely stochastic or secular processes while gradually increasing in prevalence toward cosmic noon, providing new empirical constraints on the low-mass end of black hole-galaxy coevolution.  

Radio emission from a local Little Red Dot: a challenge to the ionized cocoon scenario  
Giovanni Mazzolari, Hannah Übler, Grace E. V. Blomfield, Marcella Brusa, Jianhang Chen, Ric Davies, Francesco D'Eugenio, Frank Eisenhauer, Reinhard Genzel, Roberto Gilli, Yannick Gilmer, Shweta Jain, Xihan Ji, Ignas Juodzbalis, Dharam V. Lal, Xiaojing Lin, Roberto Maiolino, Marco Mignoli, Meghana Pannikkote, Eleonora Parlanti, Isabella Prandoni, Karla Rodriguez-Amber, Taro Shimizu, Giulia Tozzi, Bartolomeo Trefoloni  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24387v1  
Published: 2026-11-18T13:17:00Z  
Several scenarios have been proposed to explain the properties of Little Red Dots (LRDs), invoking either heavy obscuration by dense neutral gas, super-Eddington accretion, or an ionized gas cocoon that produces electron scattering. Radio observations can distinguish between these scenarios. Radio emission is largely unaffected by neutral gas and dust obscuration, whereas free-free absorption can suppress it in the presence of large free electron columns. Here, we investigate the origin of radio emission from the local LRD J1047. We jointly analyze its radio data and the broad H$α$ emission from optical spectroscopy to test the predictions of different physical scenarios. We find that the observed radio luminosity must have an AGN origin, and it is consistent with that of a standard radio-quiet AGN obscured by dense neutral gas. Pure super-Eddington accretion underpredicts the radio luminosity by a factor of $\sim 30$, although a combination of super-Eddington accretion and obscuration remains viable. Interestingly, the statistically preferred model for the H$α$ profile is an exponential fit, which is often associated with electron scattering in LRDs. This interpretation implies an electron column density of $N_e=2.4\times10^{24}\rm~cm^{-2}$ and a strong free-free absorption ($τ_{\rm ff,5GHz}\sim 10^6$), incompatible with the radio detection. This tension suggests that the exponential profiles of J1047 more likely trace a stratified broad line region rather than electron scattering, with potential implications for virial black-hole mass estimates also in high-$z$ LRDs. Less likely, the radio emission may escape through a partially covering ionized medium or originate outside the putative scattering cocoon.  

Are AGN Required? Updated Photoionization Models for Rest-UV Line Diagnostics in Reionization-era Galaxies  
Jiayang Yang, Casey Papovich, Weida Hu, Taylor A. Hutchison, Rebecca L. Larson, Pablo Arrabal Haro, Anthony J. Taylor, Ray Garner, Kaila Ronayne, Nikko J. Cleri, Steven L. Finkelstein, Bren E. Backhaus, Micaela B. Bagley, Justin W. Cole, Jeyhan S. Kartaltepe  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23391v1  
Published: 2026-11-17T19:00:22Z  
Rest-frame ultraviolet (UV) emission lines in galaxies provide important probes of the ionizing radiation, chemical abundances, and interstellar medium conditions. Here, we consider UV emission-line diagnostics for galaxies with the observed physical properties of young, metal-poor stellar populations in the epoch of reionization. We study eight $z \sim 7$--$9$ galaxies using deep JWST/NIRSpec G140M and G395M spectroscopy from the Carbon-3 plus Oxygen survey. We measure rest-frame UV and optical emission lines, $\mathrm{C\,IV}$ $λ\lambda1548,1551$, $\mathrm{He\,II}$ $\lambda1640$, $\mathrm{O\,III]}$ $\lambda1666$, $\mathrm{C\,III]}$ $λ\lambda1907,1909$, $\mathrm{[O\,II]}$ $λ\lambda3727,3730$, H$γ$, $\mathrm{[O\,III]}$ $\lambda4364$, H$β$, and $\mathrm{[O\,III]}$ $λ\lambda4960,5008$. The galaxies have low oxygen abundances, $12+\log(\mathrm{O/H})=7.50$-$7.90$, low C/O ratios, $\mathrm{C/O}=0.10$-$0.32$, and high electron densities, $n_e(\mathrm{C\,III]})=(1.9-4.9)\times10^4\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$. We compute Cloudy photoionization models using BPASS and BC03 stellar populations to test how lower C/O and higher gas density affect the UV diagnostics. We find that reducing C/O and increasing the gas density shift the star-forming grids into regions conventionally classified as composite or AGN-like. We therefore conclude that positions within these regions alone do not establish the presence of an AGN in the galaxies studied here. However, some lines remain enigmatic, including strong $\mathrm{He\,II}$, which is not reproduced by the updated models. These results highlight the need for UV diagnostic boundaries that account for the abundance patterns and gas conditions of galaxies during the epoch of reionization.
