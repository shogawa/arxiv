FALCON (FERMI-ALMA Calibrator ObservatioN): Cross-matching ALMA Calibrators with Fermi-LAT Blazars  
Kanako Narita, Yoshiyuki Inoue, Bunyo Hatsukade, Yasushi Fukazawa  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07737v1  
Published: 2026-11-05T09:47:01Z  
Blazars are active galactic nuclei with relativistic jets closely aligned with our line of sight, producing Doppler-boosted emission from radio to gamma rays. Millimeter emission near the synchrotron self-absorption turnover probes compact regions at the jet base, yet large statistical studies in this regime remain limited. We construct the FALCON (FERMI--ALMA Calibrator ObservatioN) catalogue, a millimeter-selected blazar sample by cross-matching the ALMA Calibrator Source Catalogue with the Third Data Release of the Fourth LAT AGN Catalog (4LAC-DR3). After removing duplicates, we obtain 949 unique gamma-ray-associated sources, corresponding to approximately 25 % of the 4LAC-DR3 blazar population. For the subset with available redshift measurements, we compute millimeter and gamma-ray luminosities and find a strong, statistically significant correlation that remains robust after accounting for redshift effects. Fermi-detected blazars exhibit systematically flatter millimeter spectral indices than gamma-ray non-detected ALMA calibrators, while the redshift-controlled luminosity correlation becomes progressively weaker from high-synchrotron-peaked BL Lacs toward flat-spectrum radio quasars. These results indicate an intrinsic connection between millimeter and gamma-ray emission, consistent with a common origin in relativistic jets governed by Doppler boosting and time-averaged jet power.  

Constraining the timescale of activity in Mrk 1018  
Rebecca McElroy, Scott M. Croom, Thomas Dunn  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07533v1  
Published: 2026-11-05T06:43:47Z  
We present analysis of an extended emission line region projected 3.77 kpc from the nucleus of the canonical changing-look AGN Mrk~1018, found in MUSE integral-field observations from the Close AGN Reference Survey. Emission-line ratios show that the region exhibits AGN ionisation but has a very low velocity dispersion, $77 \pm 1$ kms$^{-1}$, ruling out an ionised outflow. The lower limit on the light travel time is $12{,}300 \pm 2{,}300$ yr, implying that the nucleus has therefore been active for at least this long. This is much longer than the duration of either observed changing-look transition, which occurred over several years. This implies that Mrk 1018's multi-year variations are occurring within a longer term active phase.  

Confirming HSC strong lens candidates with DESI Spectroscopy. II. Analysis of 80 galaxy-scale systems with lens redshifts above 0.8  
Shen Li, Yiping Shu, Lin Nie  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07524v1  
Published: 2026-11-05T06:36:10Z  
The total mass distribution of galaxies provides a sensitive probe of the fundamental physical processes governing galaxy evolution, including gas inflow/star formation, AGN/SN feedback, galaxy merging, and the nature of dark matter. State-of-the-art cosmological simulations have therefore made extensive predictions for the evolution of the total-mass density slope. Interestingly, their predictions diverge significantly at high redshift, primarily due to differences in how these key physical mechanisms are implemented. However, measurements of the total-mass structure evolution have been largely confined to low and intermediate redshifts, leaving the regime where model predictions differ most significantly (z&gt;0.8) largely unexplored. In the first paper of this series, we reported the discovery of 84 candidate strong-lens systems with lens redshifts above 0.8. In this work, we perform lens modeling of the 80 galaxy-scale systems to assess their lensing nature. Of these, 71 systems (approximately 90%) exhibit clear lensing features that are well reproduced by our lens models and are therefore classified as secure strong lenses. Another eight systems are classified as probable (5) or possible (3) strong lenses, based on the prominence of their lensing features and the quality of the lens modeling. One system lacking reliable lensing features in current imaging data is classified as a non-lens. Our analysis nearly triples the number of known galaxy-scale strong lenses at z_lens &gt; 0.8, making statistical studies of the total mass structure of high-redshift galaxies possible for the first time. This expanded sample provides an unprecedented opportunity to test the predictions of cosmological simulations and discriminate between competing physical prescriptions.  

The HST-Hyperion Survey: X-ray AGN Enhancement in the Hyperion Proto-supercluster  
Ashlen Coburn, Finn Giddings, Derek Sikorski, Roy Gal, Brian C. Lemaux, Ben Forrest, Emmet Golden-Marx, Lu Shen, Devontae C. Baxter, Olga Cucciati, Joel Diamond, Denise Hung, Lori Lubin, Ekta Shah, Sandro Bardelli, Monica Natalia Isla Llave, Hyewon Suh, Laurence Tresse, Fabio Vito, Giovanni Zamorani, Elena Zucca  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07047v1  
Published: 2026-11-04T19:07:31Z  
Dense environments can influence both galaxy evolution and supermassive black hole growth, but whether they enhance or suppress (or both) active galactic nuclei (AGN) activity at high redshift ($z\gtrsim2$) remains uncertain. As one of the most massive structures identified in the early universe, the Hyperion proto-supercluster at $z\approx2.5$ provides a unique opportunity to investigate this connection during an epoch near the peak of both cosmic star formation rate and AGN activity density. To examine the impact of galaxy environment on AGN activity, we measure the fraction of X-ray-selected AGN in Hyperion as a whole, its highly overdense peaks, and its outskirts, comparing these measurements to a coeval field sample. We identify X-ray AGN using the deep Chandra COSMOS-Legacy Survey and combine optical/near-infrared (NIR) photometry and photometric redshift information from COSMOS2020 with extensive ground-based spectroscopy and HST-grism observations. Redshift information is incorporated statistically using a Monte Carlo framework. We find a higher X-ray AGN fraction in Hyperion ($7.4^{+3.2}_{-1.8}\%$) than in the coeval field ($3.2^{+0.6}_{-0.5}\%$), corresponding to a $\sim2.3σ$ difference. Strikingly, this enhancement is concentrated in the overdense peaks, where the X-ray AGN fraction reaches $12.3^{+5.5}_{-3.4}\%$ ($15.0^{+6.9}_{-4.4}\%$ in the most massive peaks versus $6.2^{+11.9}_{-2.1}\%$ in the less massive peaks), while the lower-density Hyperion outskirts ($2.1^{+3.6}_{-1.1}\%$) show no evidence of enhancement relative to the field. These results add to the limited but growing evidence for enhanced AGN activity in high-redshift protostructures, suggesting that the dense environments at these epochs may preferentially trigger AGN during the early stages of cluster assembly.  

A multi-epoch VLBI study of compact symmetric objects as contaminants in the search for milli-lenses  
Diego Álvarez-Ortega, Felix M. Pötzl, Carolina Casadio  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07029v1  
Published: 2026-11-04T19:00:18Z  
The Lambda Cold Dark Matter model predicts a high abundance of low-mass dark matter halos, which can be probed through milliarcsecond-scale gravitational lensing (milli-lensing) of background radio-loud active galactic nuclei (AGNs). The Search for MIlli-LEnses (SMILE) project aims to find these rare systems with Very Long Baseline Interferometry (VLBI). However, compact symmetric objects (CSOs) can closely resemble milli-lenses in VLBI images and are the main contaminant in such searches. We aim to determine the nature of the nine remaining milli-lens candidates of the SMILE pilot study and to characterize their physical properties. We analyzed all available multi-epoch, multi-frequency archival VLBI data, new HSA and EVN observations, and single-dish and connected-element interferometer measurements. We tested the lensing hypothesis through the morphology and spectra of the sources, and characterized their kinematics, spatially resolved spectral indices, magnetic fields, and integrated spectra, using synchrotron self-absorption (SSA) and free-free absorption (FFA) models. We identify eight of the nine candidates as CSOs, four of them for the first time. Spectral index asymmetries between components and previously undetected emission connecting them rule out the lensing hypothesis. Only J0923-3435 remains ambiguous. For the CSOs with known redshifts, the lobes have equipartition magnetic fields of $\sim$20-320 mG and projected separations of $\sim$60-240 pc, and show no measurable expansion, implying kinematic ages of at least $\sim$50-950 yr. The integrated spectra of J0210-2213 and J2347-1856 favor an inhomogeneous external free-free absorber over homogeneous and inhomogeneous SSA models. Since CSOs will be the main contaminant in the full SMILE survey, applying this framework will provide the same constraints for a much larger sample of compact radio-loud AGNs.  

AGN Archaeology: A Systematic Search for Fading AGN in MaNGA  
Quinn O. Casey, Ryan C. Hickox, Jonathan H. Cohn, Nikko J. Cleri, Julia M. Comerford, K. Decker French, Sandra Jaison, Mar Mezcua, Rogério Riffel, Dominika Wylezalek, Peixin Zhu  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07028v1  
Published: 2026-11-04T19:00:16Z  
Active galactic nuclei (AGN) vary on broad timescales, but are primarily thought to "flicker" on and off during episodes of high and low accretion. Supermassive black holes (SMBHs) which are actively accreting material often show optical spectra dominated by AGN emission in their nuclei and beyond. In this work we develop an empirical model to quantify the fractional AGN contribution to optical spectra using the Baldwin-Phillips-Terlevich (BPT) emission line diagnostic diagram. We leverage this model against integral field unit (IFU) data from the Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory (MaNGA) survey to select 34 galaxies with weak nuclear AGN activity relative to their AGN signatures at large radii; this signifies past AGN activity which is fading with time (i.e., fading-AGN). The majority of our fading-AGN reside in edge-on systems undergoing minor mergers. We construct a simple model consisting of a disk galaxy with ionization cones and find that the orientation of such a system will impact its BPT classification in both single-fiber and IFU observations. The mean distance to the extended AGN emission in our sample corresponds to a light-travel time of $1.1\times10^4$ years. We use this to set a lower-limit on the typical lifetime of the AGN phase ($t_{\text{AGN}}&gt;1.3\times10^5$ years) which is consistent with SMBHs growing via brief accretion bursts. These fading-AGN allow us to probe the elusive period of post-AGN galaxy evolution, and they give a $\sim10^4$ year baseline for understanding the accretion history in these systems.  

The obscured nucleus of NGC3256 S. I. Anisotropic AGN near-IR radiation field traced by CO v=1-0 with JWST/NIRSpec  
Miguel Pereira-Santaella, Eduardo González-Alfonso, Ismael García-Bernete, Andreas Efstathiou, Dimitra Rigopoulou, Giovanna Speranza, Niranjan Thatte  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07020v1  
Published: 2026-11-04T19:00:05Z  
The southern nucleus of NGC3256, a nearby luminous infrared (IR) interacting system (d=40 Mpc; L(IR)=10^11.6 Lsun), hosts a deeply obscured low-luminosity active galactic nucleus (AGN) associated with a massive and fast bipolar collimated molecular outflow. JWST/NIRSpec observations revealed bright spatially resolved ro-vibrational CO v=1-0 4.67um emission at the base of this molecular outflow extending out to ~300pc, which is excited by the ~4.7um IR radiation from the southern dusty core. Using Monte Carlo radiative transfer (MCRT) models, we analyzed the CO v=1-0 band emission to determine the intensity of the ~4.7um IR radiation field across the outflow base. We find that the core spectral luminosity density at ~4.7um, as seen by the material in the outflow base, is ~2-4 times higher than that inferred along our line of sight, indicating an anisotropic near-IR radiation field. Moreover, the slower than d^-2 decrease of the near-IR flux density across the outflow base suggests a collimated radiation field, for which we estimate a small half-opening angle (15-20deg) and a diameter of the collimating structure of 15 +- 5 pc. This is slightly smaller than the diameter of the obscuring envelope (25 +- 5 pc). Combined with the expansion of this obscuring envelope, traced by the blueshifted CO v=1-0 absorption toward the nucleus, this suggests that the AGN is beginning to clear its environment. We estimate N_H~10^22-23 cm^-2 for NGC 3256 S, which is similar to that of Type 2 AGN. However, in contrast to Type 2 AGN, a high covering factor of the obscuring material, possibly sustained by inflows linked to the galaxy merger, and similar to that expected in deeply obscured compact obscured nuclei (CONs), is needed to explain the absence of extended high-ionization emission. Therefore, NGC 3256 S shares properties with CONs and Type 2 AGN.
