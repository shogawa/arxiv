EP J062132.0+623837 is a new X-ray source identified in 14-day binned survey data from the Wide-field X-ray Telescope aboard the Einstein Probe (EP). Subsequent X-ray observations with the Follow-up X-ray Telescope (FXT) on board EP localized the source to the central region of the galaxy 2MASX J06213207+6238390 at $z=0.084$. Here, we present a comprehensive analysis of the multi-wavelength data for this object. We present a detailed analysis of the AGN in galaxy J085337.26+014303.6 at z=0.0582. A combination of SDSS and SAMI Galaxy Survey data shows that this object has gone through at least one full changing-look AGN cycle, from type 1.9 in 2001 to type 1 in 2015 and 2021, and then type 1.9 again in 2022. While the broad lines in this object show evidence of dust obscuration, detailed spectral analysis plus WISE photometry demonstrate that the changes in this object are related to a physical state change, not changing obscuration. In fact, the WISE light curve suggests that this object may have briefly returned to a type 1.9 state in 2020. Unresolved millimeter (mm) emission is observed in radio-quiet (RQ) Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN), but its physical origin remains debated. Self-absorbed synchrotron emission from the compact X-ray corona is one of the leading candidates. The black hole-galaxy correlations of the 12 micron local AGN sample: a NuSTAR perspective  
M. Gliozzi, L. Spinoglio, J. A. Fernandez-Ontiveros, M. A. Malkan, J. K. Williams, M. Young. A. D. Oxley, S. Satyapal  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30285v1  
Published: 2026-10-21T18:03:35Z  
The M-sigma correlation of inactive galaxies plays a crucial role in estimating the black hole mass (MBH) in active galactic nuclei (AGN): 1) it is used to calibrate the reverberation mapping (RM) method; 2) the RM is the basis of the single epoch method, the most-used technique to determine MBH of broad-line AGN; and 3) in narrow-line AGN, MBH is often directly inferred from the M-sigma correlation. The main goal of this work is to test whether the M-sigma relation calibrated from inactive galaxies can be reliably applied to a local AGN population spanning Seyfert 1, Seyfert 2, and hidden broad-line region galaxies (HBLRs), and whether the resulting AGN relation has sufficient predictive power to be used as an indirect MBH estimator. To investigate this hypothesis, we used the 12 micron AGN sample, a local representative and largely unbiased sample. Since a substantial fraction of this sample has good-quality NuSTAR data, we estimated their MBH with the X-ray scaling method, which works for any AGN accreting at moderate or high level. We find that the M-sigma correlation of the 12 micron AGN sample is different from the one obtained with inactive galaxies and has a substantial scatter. Therefore, its predictive power for MBH estimates is fairly limited. We also find that the MBH-Mgal correlation based on the 12 micron AGN sample is consistent with the analogous correlation obtained from a large sample of nearby type 1 AGN and inconsistent with that of inactive galaxies. Finally, we find suggestive evidence that Seyfert 1 and HBLRs have similar and systematically higher Eddington ratios than Seyfert 2 galaxies.

The mechanism responsible for the high-energy emission observed in kiloparsec-scale jets across cosmic time remains poorly understood. The relativistic jet associated with the z=6.1 AGN PSO J030947.49+271757.31 is the highest-redshift large-scale jet currently resolved in X-rays, providing a unique opportunity to study these structures.
