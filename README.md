Feeding or Fleeing: The Impact of Enhanced Early Black Hole Growth on Circumgalactic Clumps at $z &gt; 6$  
Floor van Donkelaar, Lucas Tortora, Debora Sijacki  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39692v1  
Published: 2026-10-29T09:31:30Z  
The circumgalactic medium (CGM) of massive high-redshift galaxies contains inflowing filamentary gas, recycled material, and feedback-driven outflows. We investigate the formation and evolution of dense CGM clumps in two cosmological zoom-in simulations of the same massive halo at $z &gt; 6$: the fiducial Fable model and the modified Fable-SE model, which includes enhanced early black hole growth through mild super-Eddington accretion. We identify dense gas clumps in the CGM and follow their gas properties, stellar content, and radial evolution across cosmic time. At the resolution probed here, the two simulations show broadly similar distributions of clump gas mass, gas size and associated stellar mass. Instead, the clump populations differ mainly in their abundance, metallicity, and radial evolution. Fable-SE produces substantially more metal-enriched clumps, including an outward-moving population during the strong AGN-driven blow-out phase at $z \lesssim 7$. These systems are associated with disturbed HI-rich gas and relatively high gas densities, linking the increased prevalence of dense, metal-enriched CGM structures in Fable-SE to its stronger AGN-driven blow-out phase. Although inward migration dominates overall, Fable-SE also contains an outward-moving clump population during the blow-out phase, with some clumps moving beyond the virial radius. Our results therefore indicate that the distribution of dense CGM clumps around massive high-redshift galaxies reflects both filamentary accretion and feedback-driven CGM evolution, with strong early AGN activity associated with the outward migration of dense, metal-enriched gas structures containing relatively cool gas.  

Towards an Astronomical Use of New-Generation Geodetic Observations  
Víctor Pérez-Díez  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39656v1  
Published: 2026-10-29T09:09:40Z  
This thesis explores the astronomical use of observations from the VLBI Global Observing System (VGOS), a next-generation geodetic network designed to improve the precision of terrestrial and celestial reference frames and Earth orientation parameters. Although primarily conceived for geodesy, its wide frequency coverage and regular monitoring of compact active galactic nuclei (AGN) provide valuable opportunities for astrophysics.   We develop a calibration pipeline that transforms VGOS correlator outputs into full-polarization, calibrated visibilities suitable for astronomical imaging. The procedure combines linear-to-circular polarization conversion, instrumental phase and amplitude calibration, and a new wideband global fringe-fitting algorithm that accounts for dispersive ionospheric effects. Regularized maximum likelihood imaging is then used to reconstruct multi-frequency intensity and polarimetric images.   The pipeline is applied to the blazar 1803+784 and five additional AGN: 3C418, 0059+581, 0613+570, 1849+670, and 2229+695. We measure frequency-dependent core shifts, spectral indices, and Faraday rotation properties. For 1803+784, the fitted core-shift index is $k_r = 0.73^{+0.12}_{-0.19}$, suggesting departures from a simple conical jet in energy equipartition.   Finally, we apply the reconstructed source models to correct structure-dependent effects in geodetic VLBI observables. The correction reduces closure-delay scatter and post-fit residuals for strongly structured sources, particularly 3C418, while its effectiveness depends on source variability and the temporal proximity of the imaging observations.   These results demonstrate the dual scientific value of VGOS as a powerful multi-frequency astronomical VLBI instrument and as a means of improving geodetic measurements.  

Migration Traps as Variability Attractors. II. Nonmonotonic X-Ray Timing Signatures of Embedded Stellar-Mass Black Holes in AGN Disks  
Jing-Tong Xing, Tong Liu, Mouyuan Sun, Zhen-Yi Cai, Ya-Ping Li, Shuying Zhou, Da-Bin Lin, Jian-Min Wang  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38432v1  
Published: 2026-10-28T08:15:14Z  
Stellar-mass black holes (sBHs) embedded in active galactic nucleus (AGN) disks may affect X-ray variability through magnetic reconnection. As sBHs migrate and accumulate near migration traps, they disturb the disk magnetic field and trigger reconnection events that heat the disk and eject plasma into the overlying corona. The injected plasma dissipates kinetic energy and can trigger the release of magnetic energy already stored in the corona. We calculate the resulting coronal X-ray emission and the disk ultraviolet (UV) variability, including the disk thermal response and reprocessing of incident X-rays. We find that embedded sBHs can initially enhance short-timescale X-ray variability, but the variability becomes suppressed as more sBHs accumulate in the disk. At higher mass accretion rates through AGN disks, this suppression appears earlier. As sBH-associated reconnection events become more frequent, their active regions increasingly overlap, reducing the magnetic energy available to each event from the corona. We also find that variations in the disk ultraviolet (UV) emission follow the X-ray variations with a shorter delay than in the model without sBHs. These results suggest that a growing population of embedded sBHs does not necessarily produce stronger short-timescale X-ray variability.

Considering Numerical Relativity Surrogate Models for AGN Channel Merger Dynamics  
Shawn Ray, Keefe Mitman, Vera Delfavero, Emily McPike, Jake Postiglione, Harrison E. Cook, Kaila Nathaniel, Nicolas Posner, Barry McKernan, K. E. Saavik Ford, Richard O'Shaughnessy  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37895v1  
Published: 2026-10-27T19:34:35Z  
Binary black hole mergers (BBH) are currently observed by the gravitational-wave detectors LIGO, Virgo, and KAGRA (LVK). A fraction of these mergers are likely to originate in active galactic nuclei (AGN), but the details of the dynamical processes driving AGN channel mergers remain poorly understood. Monte Carlo For AGN Channel Testing and Simulation (McFACTS) is an open-source, public software used to simulate AGN channel BBH formation and evolution with configurable options for exploring various AGN and Nuclear Star Cluster (NSC) parameter options. Within McFACTS, BBH mergers evolve through gas drag and dynamical interactions, ignoring relativistic effects which can influence merger remnant properties. Here we demonstrate in McFACTS the effect of handing off inspiralling BBH to a numerical relativity (NR) surrogate model, which more accurately predicts the masses, spins, and kick velocities of merger remnants. Compared to a simple analytic approach to calculating merger and remnant properties the NR surrogate model is significantly more accurate at predicting remnant spins, which improve $χ_{eff}$ and $χ_{p}$ calculations, and recoil kicks, parameters especially important for higher generation BBH mergers. Our findings show the NR surrogate significantly changes distributions of remnant spins and kick velocities, as well as showing that alternative methods should be used cautiously in future studies. We also outline some simple models which are computationally efficient and can reproduce much of the NR surrogate model results.

Extra! Extra! X-ray Emission!: X-rays in Romulus25, Part II - Physical Drivers of X-ray Emission in the CGM  
N. Nicole Sanchez, Andrew Benson, Gwen Rudie, Ben Oppenheimer, Hannah Bish, John ZuHone, Michael Tremmel  
URL: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37769v1  
Published: 2026-10-27T18:00:06Z  
Characterizing the hot (T ~ 10$^{6}$ K) circumgalactic medium (CGM) around Milky Way-mass (MW-mass) galaxies is critical to a full understanding of galaxy evolution. In this second paper of the "X-rays in Romulus25" series, we unpack the specific physical processes giving rise to the presence of hot X-ray emitting gas in the halos of galaxies from the Romulus25 (R25) simulation, focusing on L$_{*}$ galaxies. We determine that a strong correlation appears between the mass of hot CGM gas, M$_{gas, T &gt; 10^{5.5} K}$, and the X-ray emissivity, $ε_χ$, measured in the first paper of this series, (Sanchez et al. 2026a, submitted). There is also a secondary correlation between M$_{gas, T &gt; 10^{5.5} K}$ and $ε_χ$ with the average metallicity of the hot CGM gas, $\bar{Z}_{gas, T &gt; 10^{5.5} K}$. We use the well-studied PIONEER simulations from Sanchez et al. (2019), a set of zoom-in MW-mass galaxies run with the genetic modification technique of Roth et al. (2016) and the same physics as R25, to interpret these correlations. We determine that the presence of hot X-ray emitting gas is determined by two key processes: 1) the quantity of hot gas mass in the CGM, M$_{gas, T &gt; 10^{5.5} K}$, is set by the combined total energy injection of $both$ stellar and AGN feedback, and 2) together the average temperature and metallicity of the hot CGM changes the soft X-ray spectrum of the gas resulting in brighter metal-rich CGM and dimmer metal-poor CGM for the same quantity of M$_{gas, T &gt; 10^{5.5} K}$. These results indicate, for the first time, that metal-rich gas halos may bias X-ray observations of MW-mass galaxies.
